# Elijah on Mount Carmel: full script

One prophet against 450 prophets of Baal, and fire from heaven. 1 Kings 17:1 and 18. A stand-alone story from the time of the kings. Working title: *How Long Will You Limp?*

**Runtime:** about 3½ minutes (≈480 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as Noah and Jonah: the simple round PPL figures (one straight stubby arm each, dot eyes), the backdrop kit (banded skies, a dry cracked land, a mountain top with two stone altars, a crowd, fire, a storm cloud, rain), foregrounds that belong to the place. Vertical 9:16 only.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (`python scripts/fill_esv.py --bg`, then trim by hand to the "keep only" text).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** Mount Carmel isn't a magic show starring Elijah. It's God calling a people who keep "limping between two opinions" to decide who is really God, and answering a simple prayer with fire.

**Misconception it overturns:** that Elijah is a wonder-worker who wins a duel. The fire comes after one short prayer; the real challenge is to the watching crowd. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 2 (the question) and section 7.

**Spine:** Israel worships Baal, the storm god, THEREFORE God sends a drought. THEREFORE Elijah calls everyone to Mount Carmel AND asks how long they will limp between two opinions. BUT the people say nothing, THEREFORE Elijah proposes a test: the God who answers by fire is God. Baal's prophets shout all day, BUT no one answers. THEREFORE Elijah soaks his altar with water and prays, AND fire falls. THEREFORE the people cry "The LORD, he is God", AND the rain comes back.

**Re-hooks:** 0:25 ("For three years"), 0:55 ("How long"), 1:30 ("No one answered"), 2:10 ("Then Elijah prayed"), 2:45 ("And then, the rain").
