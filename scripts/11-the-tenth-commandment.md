# The Tenth Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 10 and series finale: the tenth commandment only. Exodus 20, Genesis 3, 1 Kings 21, Romans 7 and 13, Philippians 4, Hebrews 13. Working title: *You Shall Not Covet*.

**Runtime:** about 3.8 minutes (≈520 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as episode 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as episode 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (BibleGateway fill via the scratchpad esv_bg.py; trims in scripts/trim_verses.py).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The tenth commandment is the only one aimed purely at the heart; it shows that the whole law was always about love, and its cure is not more willpower but contentment in a God who will never leave.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the commandments are only about outward behaviour, so a person who never steals or cheats has kept them all. Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 5 and 7.

**Spine:** Nine commandments govern what you do, BUT the tenth governs what you want, THEREFORE no court could ever enforce it. Wanting is where breaking starts (Eden), THEREFORE it is the root of the other sins (Ahab's vineyard). THEREFORE this command works like a mirror: Paul kept the rules, BUT this one showed him his heart. THEREFORE the cure is contentment, BUT contentment has a reason: God's presence. THEREFORE all ten turn out to be one word: love.

**Re-hooks:** 0:40 ("No judge could ever catch you"), 1:20 ("It's where the very first sin began"), 2:10 ("And this commandment undid a Pharisee"), 3:15 ("And here is the end of the whole series").

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:17 is the tenth commandment. Catholic and Lutheran tradition split it into two (ninth: the neighbour's wife; tenth: his goods).
- **Partial verses.** Genesis 3:6 (up to "she took of its fruit and ate"), Romans 7:7 (from "For I would not have known") and Philippians 4:11 (from "I have learned") are trimmed with `scripts/trim_verses.py`.
- **Ahab and Naboth.** Paraphrase of 1 Kings 21:1–16 ("sulked" for "vexed and sullen", 21:4; Jezebel arranges two worthless men as false witnesses, 21:10–13). Nothing quoted; no violence shown. Chosen over David and Bathsheba to stay family friendly and because it breaks the ninth, sixth and eighth commandments in turn.
- **"Undid a Pharisee."** Paul calls himself a Pharisee and "blameless" as to righteousness under the law (Philippians 3:5–6). Romans 7:7 is his own account; many readers take it as autobiographical, as the script does.
- **Philippians from prison.** Philippians 1:13 ("my imprisonment").
- **Two tablets.** The four/six division (love God / love neighbour) is the traditional Reformed reading, attributed with "Christians have long read". Part 1 used Matthew 22:37; this finale uses Romans 13:9–10, which itself lists "You shall not covet".
