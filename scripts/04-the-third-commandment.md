# The Third Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 3: the third commandment only. Exodus 3 and 20, Leviticus 19, Numbers 6, Matthew 6. Working title: *Carrying the Name*.

**Runtime:** about 3.5 minutes (≈500 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as the first commandment.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as episode 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (BibleGateway fill, then scripts/trim_verses.py).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The third commandment is not only about swearing; God gave his people his name, and the command is not to carry that name emptily, in word or in life.

**Misconception it overturns:** that "taking the name in vain" just means using God's name as a swear word. Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 5 and 6.

**Spine:** God gave Israel his own name at the burning bush, THEREFORE his name is a gift and a closeness. THEREFORE at Sinai he guards it: do not "take" it "in vain". BUT "take" means lift or carry and "in vain" means empty, THEREFORE it covers careless words and false oaths. BUT Israel also wore the name (Numbers 6:27), like a bride takes a name, THEREFORE you can break it without saying a word. THEREFORE Jesus teaches us to pray first that the name be hallowed.

**Re-hooks:** 0:20 ("You can break this commandment without saying a word"), 1:25 ("Look closely at two words"), 2:20 ("But here's where it gets bigger"), 3:10 ("Centuries later").

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:7 is the third commandment (20:3 first, 20:4–6 second). In the Catholic and Lutheran count this is the second.
- **Partial verses.** Exodus 3:14 is read as "I AM WHO I AM." only; Exodus 3:15 is read from "This is my name forever"; Matthew 6:9 from "Our Father in heaven". Rules in scripts/trim_verses.py.
- **Hebrew.** "Take" is *nasa'* (lift, carry, bear); "in vain" is *lashshav'* (emptiness, falsehood, worthlessness). The divine name is the four letters YHWH, conventionally pronounced Yahweh; English Bibles print it LORD in small capitals. Its link with "I am" (Exodus 3:14, *'ehyeh*) is the common scholarly reading; the script only sets the two side by side.
- **"Bride" image.** An illustration, attributed ("Many readers hear the third commandment that way"); the "carrying the name" reading leans on *nasa'* plus Numbers 6:27 and Deuteronomy 28:10 ("called by the name of the LORD"). It is offered alongside, not instead of, the traditional reading about speech and oaths.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** "Burned but did not burn up" paraphrases Exodus 3:2; the priestly blessing is Numbers 6:24–26 (not quoted). "Hallowed means treated as holy" is a definition, not scripture.
- **Matthew 6:9.** Linking the first petition to the third commandment is a common Christian reading (attributed), not something Jesus says.
- **Application** in sections 4 and 6 is the narrator's.
