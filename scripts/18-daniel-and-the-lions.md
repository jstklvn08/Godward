# Daniel and the Lions: full script

An old man keeps praying when it's against the law, and is thrown to the lions. Daniel 6. A stand-alone story from Israel's exile in Babylon. Working title: *As He Had Done Before*.

**Runtime:** about 3½ minutes (≈500 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as Gideon and Job: the simple round PPL figures (one straight stubby arm each, dot eyes), the backdrop kit (banded skies, a city of ziggurats, palace columns, an upper room with an open window, foregrounds that belong to the place), SC lions. Vertical 9:16 only.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (`python scripts/fill_esv.py --bg`, then trim by hand to the "keep only" text).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** Daniel didn't become brave the night he faced the lions. He had prayed three times a day for years, and he simply kept doing it. God's rescue met a faithfulness that was already there.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the story is about a fearless hero taming lions. Daniel was over eighty, and the point is a quiet habit of prayer. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 3 ("as he had done previously") and section 7.

**Spine:** Daniel serves the king faithfully, BUT the other officials are jealous, THEREFORE they trick the king into a law against prayer. BUT Daniel prays anyway, as he always had, THEREFORE he is caught and thrown to the lions. BUT God shuts the lions' mouths, THEREFORE in the morning Daniel walks out unharmed, AND the king tells the whole empire about Daniel's God.

**Re-hooks:** 0:25 ("Daniel was an old man"), 1:05 ("So they set a trap"), 1:35 ("Daniel heard about the law"), 2:15 ("That night"), 2:50 ("At dawn").
