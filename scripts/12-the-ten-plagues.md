# The Ten Plagues of Egypt: full script

The ten plagues and the first Passover. Exodus 5, 7–12, with 1 Corinthians 5:7. It leads into the Ten Commandments series: rescue first, then the sea, then Sinai. Working title: *Who Is the LORD?*

**Runtime:** about 4¼ minutes (≈600 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as the Ten Commandments episodes: the simple round PPL figures (straight stubby arms, dot eyes), SC scenery, FX fire and smoke. Vertical 9:16 only.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV via scripts/fill_esv.py.

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The plagues weren't random disasters. They were God's answer to Pharaoh's question, "Who is the LORD?", aimed one by one at the gods Egypt trusted. They end in a rescue through the blood of a lamb.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the plagues are just an angry God hitting Egypt over and over. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 6 ("on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments").

**Spine:** Pharaoh asks "Who is the LORD?", THEREFORE God answers with signs. BUT Pharaoh's heart is hardened, THEREFORE the signs grow. BUT God draws a line around his people, THEREFORE it's clear who is acting. THEREFORE each plague strikes something Egypt worshipped: the river, the land, the cattle, the sky, the sun. BUT the last plague would reach Israel too, THEREFORE God gives a way through: the blood of a lamb. THEREFORE Pharaoh lets them go, and Christians see in that night a picture of Christ.

**Re-hooks:** 0:25 ("That question is the whole story"), 1:45 ("That's the turn"), 2:55 ("So why ten?"), 3:20 ("The last plague was the heaviest").

## 0. Cold open: a river turns red (0:00–0:25)

**Visual:** Night-blue sheet, the Nile running through reeds under a pale moon. The water darkens to red from upstream down, the reflection of the moon turns red with it. Quick cut-ins: frogs on a palace step, hail with fire inside it, then the frame goes black except for one lit window.
**On screen:** Black label: **TEN PLAGUES**, then **A CONTEST?** (one label at a time)
**Narration:**
> [grave] A river turns to blood. [beat]
> [story] Frogs fill the palace. Hail falls with fire inside it. And then comes a darkness you can feel.
> [talk] Most people remember the ten plagues as a list of disasters. An angry God, hitting Egypt again and again.
> [pivot] But read it closely, and it's something else. [beat]
> [talk] It's a contest. And Egypt's gods are losing.

**Transition:** Cut to a sheet of brick red.

## 1. Who is the LORD? (0:25–0:55)

**Visual:** Brick-making slaves bent under a red sky, a taskmaster as a long shadow on the ground (never a figure with a whip). Then Pharaoh's hall: a tall throne, columns, a big sun disc on the wall. Moses (an old man with a staff) and Aaron stand small before the throne. Pharaoh leans back and laughs.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 5:2}} (keep only up to "let Israel go?") · EXODUS 5:2 ESV
**Narration:**
> [story] For generations, Israel had been slaves in Egypt.
> [story] Then God sent Moses to Pharaoh, the most powerful king on earth, with one message: let my people go.
> [story] Pharaoh laughed.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 5:2}}
> [pivot] That question is the whole story. [beat]
> [talk] Ten times, God is going to answer it.

## 2. The river (0:55–1:20)

**Visual:** Wide Nile at dawn, boats, people fetching water, a carved river god on the bank. Moses' staff strikes the water; red spreads out from it. Dead fish float, people step back holding their noses, jars of red water. The river god statue cracks.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 7:20}} (keep only "and all the water in the Nile turned into blood") · EXODUS 7:20 ESV · label **THE NILE**
**Narration:**
> [story] First, the Nile. It was Egypt's lifeline. They worshipped it, because it fed the whole country.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 7:20}}
> [story] The fish died. The river stank. And the god of the Nile could do nothing.
> [talk] Pharaoh's magicians copied the trick, so Pharaoh shrugged it off. His heart was hard, and he would not listen.

## 3. Frogs, gnats, flies (1:20–1:45)

**Visual:** Montage, one sheet per beat with cuts. Frogs everywhere in the palace: on the bed, in the bread oven, on the steps (big recognisable frogs, a little comic). A statue with a frog's head stands among them. Then dust rising into clouds of tiny gnats; the magicians, flustered, put their staffs down. Then a dark swarm of flies over Egyptian houses that stops at a clear line: on the other side, Goshen, green and quiet.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 8:19}} (keep only "This is the finger of God.") · EXODUS 8:19 ESV · label **GOSHEN**
**Narration:**
> [story] Then frogs. In the ovens, in the beds, in the king's own bedroom. Egypt had a goddess with the head of a frog. Now her frogs were everywhere.
> [story] Then gnats, from the dust of the ground. This time the magicians failed, and they told Pharaoh so.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 8:19}}
> [story] Then swarms of flies. [beat]
> [pivot] But not in Goshen, where Israel lived.

## 4. A line between (1:45–2:15)

**Visual:** A split sheet. On the Egyptian side cattle lie down in a dry field; on Israel's side the herds graze in green grass, a shepherd and his children among them. Then the magicians, covered in red boil dots (stylised, not gory), turn away from Moses.
**On screen:** Label **A LINE BETWEEN**
**Narration:**
> [talk] That's the turn. God draws a line between his people and Egypt.
> [story] Egypt's livestock die in the fields. Israel's are spared.
> [story] Then boils break out on people and animals alike. The magicians can't even stand in front of Moses.
> [talk] And still, Pharaoh's heart is hard.

## 5. Hail, locusts, darkness (2:15–2:55)

**Visual:** A storm sheet: huge hail with fire flickering inside it, barley flattened, people running under roofs. Then the sky turns brown with locusts and the fields go bare. Then the darkness: the whole Egyptian city black, a single sun disc on a temple roof gone dark, while across the river Israel's houses glow with lamplight.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 10:22-23}} (keep only "and there was pitch darkness in all the land of Egypt three days" … "but all the people of Israel had light where they lived") · EXODUS 10:22–23 ESV
**Narration:**
> [grave] Then hail, with fire flashing through it. The worst storm Egypt had ever seen.
> [story] Locusts eat whatever the hail left. Not one green thing remains.
> [grave] And then, darkness.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 10:22-23}}
> [talk] Egypt's greatest god was the sun. For three days, it was simply gone.

## 6. Why ten? (2:55–3:20)

**Visual:** Back to a dark paper sheet. Nine cut-outs pinned in a row: river, frog, cow, sky, sun, and the rest. Each one gets a coral X as it's named. A tenth pin is left empty, waiting.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 12:12}} (keep only "and on all the gods of Egypt I will execute judgments: I am the LORD.") · EXODUS 12:12 ESV
**Narration:**
> [pivot] So why ten? Why not one plague, and done? [beat]
> [talk] God says why himself.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 12:12}}
> [talk] Every plague was aimed at something Egypt trusted. The river. The land. The cattle. The sky. The sun.
> [talk] One by one, each was shown to be powerless.

## 7. The last night (3:20–4:00)

**Visual:** Night in an Israelite village. A father brushes red onto the doorframe with a bunch of hyssop; his children watch, the mother holds a lamp. Inside, the family eats standing up, sandals on, bags packed. Outside, a cold wind and a shadow sweep across the Egyptian rooftops and pass by the marked doors (God and death are never shown as a figure: wind, shadow, the door lamps flickering). Then dawn, and Egyptians pushing gold and bread into the Israelites' hands as they leave.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 12:13}} (keep only "And when I see the blood, I will pass over you") · EXODUS 12:13 ESV · label **PASSOVER**
**Narration:**
> [grave] The last plague was the heaviest. At midnight, death would pass through the land, to every firstborn son.
> [story] But God gave Israel a way through. Each family killed a lamb, and painted its blood on the doorframe.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 12:13}}
> [story] That night, a great cry went up across Egypt. But behind the doors marked with blood, every child was safe.
> [story] By morning, Pharaoh was begging them to leave.

## 8. The answer (4:00–4:30)

**Visual:** Dawn over the desert: the long line of Israel walking out, children on shoulders, animals with them, the sea far ahead as a band of blue. The torn strip with Exodus 5:2 drifts back in and the coral marker circles "Who is the LORD". End card: a lamb's silhouette on a hill at sunrise, then the two stone tablets faintly on the horizon (the Ten Commandments series).
**On screen:** {{ESV 1 Corinthians 5:7}} (keep only "For Christ, our Passover lamb, has been sacrificed.") · 1 CORINTHIANS 5:7 ESV · end card: ESV credit
**Narration:**
> [talk] Remember Pharaoh's question? Who is the LORD? [beat]
> [talk] Now all of Egypt knew. And so did Israel.
> [talk] Centuries later, Christians looked back at that night and saw a picture of something bigger.
> [grave] {{ESV 1 Corinthians 5:7}}
> [story] The rescue came first, through the blood of a lamb. Then came the sea. And then, a mountain, where God would speak.
> [pivot] But that's the next story.
