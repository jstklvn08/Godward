# Creation and the Fall: full script

Episode 1, Genesis 1–3. Working title: *How Everything Went Wrong in Eden*.

**Runtime:** about 8 minutes (≈1,150 narrated words including the scripture readings, at 140–145 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** the glowing-collage style of `videos/creation-and-the-fall/6-vox-kurzgesagt-mix.mp4`.
**Voice:** one narrator, calm and curious, with short pauses before the turns.
**Translation:** English Standard Version (ESV). See "Scripture text" below.

## Scripture text

Scripture appears in this file as references, written `{{ESV Genesis 1:1}}`, not as typed-out text. Run `scripts/fill_esv.py` to produce `01-creation-and-the-fall.esv.md` with the ESV wording filled in from the official ESV API. Always use the filled copy for recording and on-screen text, so every quotation comes straight from the ESV and nothing is retyped from memory.

Each placeholder brings in the whole verse. Where a scene only needs part of a verse, the note beside it says which part to keep; trim it in the filled copy.

## Style rules for every scene

- **The wall.** The story runs along one wall of taped paper sheets. Each scene is a sheet, and the camera slides sideways between sheets, with torn edges and a dark gap showing in the pan.
- **Paper and grain.** Deep indigo, teal and crimson paper, grain and fibre texture, and light that sits on top of the print as a soft glow.
- **Characters.** Adam and Eve are round, simple figures with dot eyes, white sticker borders, a soft halftone shade and a faint aura. They move in held poses, on twos. They wear simple tunics throughout.
- **Scripture.** Quotes appear on torn cream strips with tape, in serif type, with a yellow highlighter sweeping across the key words. The reference sits in a black label box with bold white sans type, followed by "ESV".
- **Annotations.** Coral marker circles and arrows, black labels and neon lines, as in the map scene.
- **God.** Never shown as a person. God's speech appears as light and as words on paper.
- **Light before the sun.** The sun, moon and stars appear only on Day 4. Before then there is pure light with no visible source.
- **Cherubim.** Shown abstractly, as wings of light.
- **The serpent.** A glowing magenta serpent with a sticker outline. It is the only thing that keeps its glow after the fall.

Scripture is quoted exactly from the ESV. Everything else is paraphrase and is never presented as scripture.

## 0. Cold open: the guarded gate (0:00–0:25)

**Visual:** The last sheet, shown first. A huge paper gate glows from inside. A flaming sword turns in front of it, with wings of light on either side. Two small figures walk away to the left. The camera holds, then a coral marker circles the sword.

**On screen:** Black label: **WHY IS PARADISE GUARDED?**

**Narration:**
> At the edge of the oldest story in the Bible, there's a gate. On one side, a garden. On the other, two people walking away. And between them, a guard: a flaming sword, turning every way. How did it come to this? To find out, we have to go back. All the way back.

**Transition:** The camera whips left along the wall, past several sheets, to the first one.

## 2. Six days of order (0:50–2:05)

**Visual:** One sheet split into six panels like a field notebook, numbered in black labels. Each panel lights up as its day is named.
- **Day 1:** Pure light floods the panel from no source. No sun.
- **Day 2:** A neon line splits the waters above from the waters below.
- **Day 3:** Paper land rises out of the sea, and glowing plants sprout.
- **Day 4:** Only now do the sun, moon and stars appear, as glowing paper discs.
- **Day 5:** Fish and birds fill the sea and the sky.
- **Day 6:** Animals, then two small glowing figures.

Coral brackets join Days 1–3 ("SPACES") to Days 4–6 ("FILLERS").

**On screen:**
- {{ESV Genesis 1:3}} · GENESIS 1:3 ESV
- {{ESV Genesis 1:27}} (keep the first clause) · GENESIS 1:27 ESV
- {{ESV Genesis 1:31}} (keep the end of the verse, about everything being very good) · GENESIS 1:31 ESV

**Narration:**
> Then God speaks.

> {{ESV Genesis 1:3}}

> Light comes first, three whole days before the sun. Light here doesn't come from anything. It comes from a word.

> What follows is a week of order out of chaos. On days one to three, God makes spaces: light and dark, sky and sea, dry land. On days four to six, he fills them: lights to rule the day and night, fish and birds, and finally the animals of the land.

> Then the rhythm breaks. For the last creature, God doesn't just say "Let there be". He says: let us make man.

> {{ESV Genesis 1:27}}

> Male and female. Not the biggest creatures, not the strongest, but the only ones said to carry his image.

> And at the end of it all, God looks over everything he's made, and it is very good. Not just good. Very good. On the seventh day, he rests.

**Transition:** The camera slides right to the next sheet. The six panels fold away.

> So where was Eden? Honestly, nobody knows. The other two rivers can't be identified, and people have suggested sites from Mesopotamia to Armenia. The map can point us toward a region. It can't put a pin in it.

> But the picture is clear enough: a place full of life, with water and fruit, and with God close by.

**Narration:**
> Then a new character slides into the story.

> {{ESV Genesis 3:1}} (read the first sentence only)

> And it opens not with a lie, but with a question. Did God really say you can't eat from any tree in the garden? It takes God's one "no" and makes it sound like a thousand.

> The woman corrects it, and then the serpent goes further. Put the two sentences side by side. God said you must not eat of it. The serpent says:

> {{ESV Genesis 3:4}}

> Same little word, moved one place.

> And then it offers a reason. If you eat, your eyes will be opened, and you'll be like God, knowing good and evil. The real temptation isn't fruit. It's the idea that God is holding something back.

## Notes for production

- **Translation and permission.** All scripture is from the ESV, which is under copyright to Crossway. Show the copyright notice on screen and in the video description, and check Crossway's and the ESV API's current terms before the channel is monetised. See the same note in `ten-commandments-1-no-other-gods.md`.
- **Words the script relies on.** Section 5 and section 7 depend on the word "not" appearing in both God's command (Genesis 2:17) and the serpent's reply (Genesis 3:4). Section 8's highlighter assumes Genesis 3:6 uses "ate". Section 9's title assumes Genesis 3:9's question reads "Where are you?". Check the filled text and adjust the highlighted words and the section title to match if they differ.
- **Paraphrased lines.** Several lines retell a verse in the narrator's words instead of quoting it: the flaming sword (Genesis 3:24) in the cold open, "let us make man" (1:26), "very good" (1:31), the garden in the east (2:8), the serpent's question (3:1), "you'll be like God" (3:5), the fruit's appeal (3:6), the eyes opened (3:7), "the way to the tree of life" (3:24). These are paraphrase, not quotation, so they aren't set in quotation marks.
- **Partial verses.** Where a scene note says to keep or read part of a verse, trim the filled text and keep the reference on screen. Check after filling that the kept part still reads as a complete phrase.
- **Rivers.** Older translations, including the KJV, name the third river "Hiddekel". The ESV translates it as "Tigris". The map label keeps both.
- **Nakedness and clothing.** Genesis 2:25 and 3:7 speak of nakedness. To keep Adam and Eve in simple clothing, the script quotes only the first clause of 3:7 and shows shame through hiding and dimming.
- **Interpretive claims are labelled.** "Later writers would connect it with Satan" refers to texts such as Revelation 12:9. "Christians have long read this as the first hint of a rescue" refers to the traditional reading of Genesis 3:15. The script attributes both rather than stating them as Genesis's own words.
- **"Let us make."** Genesis 1:26 uses this phrase. The script notes the change of rhythm and doesn't argue for any one explanation of the plural.
- **Existing clip.** Scenes 4, 7, 8 and 11 reuse the layouts already built in `6-vox-kurzgesagt-mix.html`. The rest are new sheets in the same style. The built clips still show KJV wording on screen; update their text to the filled ESV wording before reusing them.
