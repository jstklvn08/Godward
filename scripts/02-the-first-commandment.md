# The First Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 1: the first commandment only. Exodus 14, 19–20 and 32, Psalm 115, Matthew 22. Working title: *No Other Gods*.

**Runtime:** about 4 minutes (≈580 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as episode 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as episode 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV via scripts/fill_esv.py.

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The first commandment isn't a rule for earning God's love; it is what follows from being rescued by him: no one else gets a place before his face.

**Misconception it overturns:** that "no other gods" just means "don't join another religion" or "put God first on your list". Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 5 and 8.

**Spine:** God rescues Israel, THEREFORE he speaks, and he starts with who he is, not with a rule. THEREFORE "no other gods" means no backup, no divided trust. BUT "before me" means "before my face", THEREFORE it is a relationship, not a ranking. BUT forty days later Israel breaks it by adding a god, not by leaving God, THEREFORE we see it is about trust. THEREFORE Jesus states it from the inside: love God with all your heart.

**Re-hooks:** 0:55 ("Here's the strange thing"), 1:50 ("And look at that little word"), 2:25 ("And Israel broke it fast"), 3:25 ("Centuries later").

**Visual:** Close on the word "before" in the strip. It lifts off the paper and turns into a glowing outline of a face-shaped light (not a human face: a soft oval of light). A small cut-out idol slides into the frame beside it and is pushed back out. Black label: **NOT A RANKING. A RELATIONSHIP.**
**On screen:** Label: **BEFORE = "TO MY FACE"** (Hebrew *'al-panay*, from *panim*, "face")

> [talk] It isn't “God first, then everything else in order.” It's “no one else in the room.” [pause]
> [story] The first commandment isn't a ranking. It's a relationship.

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Follows the Reformed/Orthodox count (20:3 is the first commandment, 20:4–6 images the second). Jewish tradition counts 20:2 as the first "word"; Catholic and Lutheran tradition join 20:3–6. If the series wants to stay neutral, change the end card to **NEXT: NO CARVED IMAGE**.
- **Partial verses.** Exodus 14:30 (first clause), Exodus 32:4 (the people's words) and Matthew 22:37 (Jesus' words) are read and shown in part; after filling, trim them in the filled file with `scripts/trim_verses.py`.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** "A feast to the LORD" is Exodus 32:5; "three months later" is Exodus 19:1; "forty days" is Exodus 24:18; "got tired of waiting" paraphrases Exodus 32:1. None are shown as verse cards.
- **"Before" and "face".** Exodus 20:3 reads *'al-panay*, built on *panim*, "face". Translations render it "before me" or "besides me". The script claims only the root. Confirm after filling that the ESV reads "before me".
- **Egypt's gods.** Sun, river, harvest and king are generic and illustrative; Exodus 12:12 speaks of judgement on "all the gods of Egypt" without naming them.
- **Matthew 22:37–38.** Jesus quotes Deuteronomy 6:5 and calls it "the great and first commandment". The script presents it as the first commandment stated positively; that link is the narrator's reading ("He turned the first one inside out"), not a claim that Jesus cited Exodus 20:3.
- **Application** in section 7 is the narrator's.
