# The Fifth Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 5: the fifth commandment only. Exodus 20, Ephesians 6, Psalm 27 and John 19. Working title: *Give Them Weight*.

**Runtime:** about 4 minutes (≈540 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as episode 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as episode 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (filled from BibleGateway's ESV text).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The fifth commandment is the hinge between loving God and loving your neighbor: to honor your parents is to give them weight, which is not the same as approving of everything they did, and Jesus kept it even from the cross.

**Misconception it overturns:** that "honor your father and your mother" is a rule for small children ("do what you're told"). Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 2 and 7.

**Spine:** The first four commandments are about God, THEREFORE the turn to the neighbor starts at home. "Honor" means "give weight", THEREFORE it outlasts childhood obedience. It carries a promise, THEREFORE it matters for whole families and peoples. BUT some parents are hard to honor, THEREFORE honor is not pretending, and the weight runs both ways. BUT some parents leave, THEREFORE God takes in the abandoned. THEREFORE Jesus, dying, still gives his mother a home, and the command looks like small, concrete acts now.

**Re-hooks:** 0:55 ("So what does honor actually mean?"), 1:30 ("And this command comes with something"), 2:00 ("But what if your parents were hard to honor?"), 3:00 ("And then there's the cross.").

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:12 is the fifth commandment, Exodus 20:13 the sixth.
- **Spelling.** Narration and labels use "honor" and "neighbor" to match the ESV verse cards.
- **Two tablets.** "Two halves" is attributed ("Many readers"). The love-God / love-neighbor summary echoes Matthew 22:37–39 and is paraphrase, not shown as a verse card. (Some Jewish tradition, e.g. Philo, divides the tablets five and five, putting this command with the God-ward ones; the narration no longer mentions it, but it supports the "hinge" picture.)
- **Honor / heavy / glory.** "Honor" in Exodus 20:12 is *kabbed* (Piel of *kabed*, "to be heavy"); "glory" is *kavod*, same root.
- **The promise.** Paul's words in Ephesians 6:2–3 are shown whole (they include his parenthesis). The wider reading of the promise is attributed ("Christians have long read it more widely").
- **Hard or absent parents.** Section 4 is careful pastoral framing: "honor is not pretending" and "respect the place a parent holds, while grieving what they did with it" are attributed to "many Christian teachers"; "staying in harm's way" makes clear the command does not ask anyone to remain in danger. Ephesians 6:4 shows the obligation runs both ways. "Whoever let you go, God does not" is the narrator's application of Psalm 27:10.
- **John 19:26–27.** Read whole. "Watching" is narrative colour, not quotation. "He kept this commandment to the very end" is the narrator's reading. Jesus is not shown as a figure; the cross is empty paper silhouette and his words arrive on paper in light.
- **Next episode pointer.** The sixth commandment's words (Exodus 20:13) are not typed in the narration; it is named only as "the sixth commandment".
- **Application** in section 7 is the narrator's.
