# Esther: full script

A young Jewish woman becomes queen of Persia and risks her life to save her people. The book of Esther. A stand-alone story from the exile, a generation after Daniel. Working title: *For Such a Time as This*.

**Runtime:** about 3¾ minutes (≈540 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as Daniel: the simple round PPL figures (one straight stubby arm each, dot eyes), the backdrop kit (banded skies, Persian palace halls with columns and blue glazed walls, a throne with a gold sceptre, banquet tables, the city of Susa), foregrounds that belong to the place. Vertical 9:16 only.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (`python scripts/fill_esv.py --bg`, then trim by hand to the "keep only" text).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** God's name doesn't appear once in the book of Esther, and yet nothing in it is an accident. God works through ordinary people and "coincidences", and calls them to be brave at the moment that matters.

**Misconception it overturns:** that Esther is a fairy tale about a girl who won a beauty contest. It's about courage under risk, and a God who is hidden but never absent. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 5 (the sleepless night) and section 7.

**Spine:** Esther, an orphan, becomes queen, BUT she hides that she is Jewish. Haman hates the Jews, THEREFORE he tricks the king into a law to destroy them all. THEREFORE Mordecai asks Esther to go to the king, BUT going in uninvited could mean death. BUT "who knows… for such a time as this", THEREFORE she fasts and goes, AND the king holds out his sceptre. THEREFORE, after a sleepless night and two banquets, she tells the king the truth, AND the day of mourning becomes a feast.

**Re-hooks:** 0:25 ("Esther was an orphan"), 1:00 ("Then came Haman"), 1:35 ("Mordecai sent a message"), 2:15 ("On the third day"), 2:45 ("That night, the king couldn't sleep").
