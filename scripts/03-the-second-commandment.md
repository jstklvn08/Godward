# The Second Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 2: the second commandment only. Exodus 20:4–6, with Isaiah 44, Deuteronomy 4 and Genesis 1. Condensed from the longer draft `scripts/ten-commandments-2-no-graven-image.md`. Working title: *What's Wrong With a Statue of God?*

**Runtime:** about 4 minutes (≈540 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as episode 1 and part 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as part 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (filled from BibleGateway's ESV text; see notes).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** An image of God makes him too small and too quiet. At Sinai God gave Israel a voice to listen to, not a shape to look at, and he had already put his true image on earth: people.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the second commandment is just a ban on other religions' idols, a repeat of the first. Named in section 1 ("that was the first commandment's job"), paid off in sections 4 and 6.

**Spine:** Isaiah mocks a man who prays to half a log, BUT behind the joke is a real question, THEREFORE we read the command: no carved image of anything in creation. BUT why? THEREFORE back to Sinai: they saw no form, only a voice. THEREFORE an image shrinks God and silences him (the calf, as a callback, was an attempt to hold the LORD). BUT God already has an image on earth, people. AND YET we still carve gods in our heads, THEREFORE the command ends with a warning outweighed by mercy.

**Re-hooks:** 0:25 ("What's actually wrong with a statue of God?"), 1:15 ("Why be so strict about it?"), 1:55 ("So here's the problem with a statue"), 2:40 ("But here's the surprise"), 3:20 ("The commandment ends with a warning").

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Orthodox count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:4–6 is the second commandment; Catholic and Lutheran tradition join it to the first. Exodus 20:7 (the LORD's name) is next.
- **Partial verses.** Isaiah 44:17 is read from "He prays to it" (his prayer); Exodus 20:5 is read only to "or serve them" (trim rules in `scripts/trim_verses.py`). Exodus 20:6 begins mid-sentence ("but showing steadfast love"); it is read whole, following the narrator's paraphrase of 20:5b.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** The cold open paraphrases Isaiah 44:15–16; "the third and fourth generation" paraphrases Exodus 20:5b; "a feast to the LORD" is Exodus 32:5 (callback to part 1). None are shown as verse cards.
- **Deuteronomy 4.** The draft pointed to 4:15 ("you saw no form"); 4:12 says the same in a complete sentence ("saw no form; there was only a voice") and is used instead. 4:15–19 then draws the image ban from it.
- **"Notice the list"** linking Exodus 20:4 to creation is the narrator's reading (Deuteronomy 4:16–19 spells out the same range).
- **Genesis 1:27** uses a different Hebrew word for "image" (*tselem*) than Exodus 20:4 (*pesel*); the link is thematic and attributed ("Christians have long read it this way").
- **"Thousands"** can be read as thousands of people or thousands of generations (compare Deuteronomy 7:9); the contrast holds either way.
