# The Eighth Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 8: the eighth commandment only. Exodus 20, Joshua 6–7, Leviticus 19, Luke 19, Ephesians 4. Working title: *Opposite Hands*.

**Runtime:** about 4 minutes (≈520 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as part 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as part 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (filled from BibleGateway's ESV; see the series brief).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** "You shall not steal" guards what God has given to each neighbor, and its opposite is not just keeping your hands to yourself but opening them: generosity.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the eighth commandment is the easy one, because "I've never robbed anybody". Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 3, 6 and 7.

**Spine:** God gives every family in Israel a share of the land, THEREFORE stealing is grabbing what God gave someone else. BUT the first theft in the land (Achan) starts in the heart and ends hidden, THEREFORE most stealing is small and hidden. BUT God's law goes further than "don't take": leave the edges for the poor, THEREFORE holding on too tightly is its own kind of taking. THEREFORE when Zacchaeus meets Jesus in the same city, his hands turn the other way. THEREFORE Paul ends the command with "share".

**Re-hooks:** 0:55 ("And the first time Israel entered that land"), 2:10 ("But here's the surprising part"), 2:55 ("Centuries later, Jesus walked into Jericho"), 3:40 ("Notice where that sentence ends").

## 0. Cold open: four words (0:00–0:25)

**Visual:** A charcoal sheet. The two stone tablets stand in the dark, ten carved lines glowing faintly; the eighth line lights up. Then a pair of glowing cut-out hands, closed into fists, at the bottom of the frame. A coral marker writes a question mark beside them.
**On screen:** Black label: **THE EASY ONE?** · then {{ESV Exodus 20:15}} · EXODUS 20:15 ESV · later label **WHAT ARE YOUR HANDS FOR?**
**Narration:**
> [pivot] It's one of the shortest commandments in the whole Bible.
> [story] In English, it's just four words.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 20:15}}
> [talk] Most of us read that and relax. We've never robbed a bank.
> [talk] So this one feels easy. [beat]
> [pivot] But the Bible asks a much bigger question. Not only “what did you take?” But “what are your hands for?”

**Transition:** The camera slides down the wall to a sheet of green-gold land.

## 1. Every family got a share (0:25–0:55)

**Visual:** A torn paper map of the land, cut into plots by little boundary stones. On each plot a small glowing family, and a cut-out of what they own: a field of wheat, a vineyard, a flock of sheep. A gold light passes over the map, plot by plot.
**On screen:** Labels **A FIELD**, **A VINEYARD**, **A FLOCK** · black label **WHAT GOD GAVE YOUR NEIGHBOR**
**Narration:**
> [story] When Israel heard these words at Sinai, they were a nation of freed slaves. They owned almost nothing.
> [story] But God was bringing them to a land. And every family would get a share of it. A field. A vineyard. A flock.
> [talk] So when God says “do not steal,” he is guarding what he gave to your neighbor.
> [talk] Stealing grabs what God handed to someone else.

## 2. Achan (0:55–1:45)

**Visual:** A dusty ochre sheet: the fallen walls of Jericho. A pile of treasure glows: a beautiful red cloak, silver coins, a bar of gold. One small figure (Achan) stops beside it. Four coral words appear one by one as the narration names them: SAW, WANTED, TOOK, HID. The treasure slides down into a hole under a tent; the tent's glow dims.
**On screen:** {{ESV Joshua 7:21}} (keep from "I coveted them" to the end) · JOSHUA 7:21 ESV · coral words SAW / WANTED / TOOK / HID
**Narration:**
> [pivot] And the first time Israel entered that land, someone broke it.
> [story] At Jericho, God told them that everything in the city was devoted to him. Nobody was to keep any of it.
> [story] But a man named Achan saw a beautiful cloak, and some silver, and a bar of gold.
> [story] Later, he confessed.
> [grave] {{ESV Joshua 7:21}}
> [talk] Listen to the order. He saw. He wanted. He took. He hid. [pause]
> [talk] The theft happened with his hands. But it started in his heart.

## 3. The tent we hide things in (1:45–2:10)

**Visual:** The tent from section 2 becomes a modern cut-out room under the same tent roof. Small paper objects slide in and are tucked under the floor: a homework page with someone else's name, a few extra coins of change, a gold star "credit" sticker. Black label: **SMALL. HIDDEN. EASY TO EXPLAIN.**
**On screen:** Labels **HOMEWORK**, **EXTRA CHANGE**, **CREDIT**
**Narration:**
> [talk] Most stealing doesn't look like a robbery. It looks like Achan's tent: small, hidden, and easy to explain.
> [story] Copying someone's homework. Keeping the extra change. Taking credit you didn't earn.
> [talk] Nobody sees it, but it's still stealing.

## 4. Leave the edges (2:10–2:55)

**Visual:** A warm wheat-gold sheet: a field seen from above, rows of grain. The reaper's line stops short of the edges; the corners stay standing and glowing. Grapes left on the vine. Small glowing figures (a widow, a traveler, a child) come to the edges and gather. Black label: **LEFT ON PURPOSE.**
**On screen:** label **THE EDGES** · then {{ESV Leviticus 19:10}} (keep the second sentence, "You shall leave them...") · LEVITICUS 19:10 ESV
**Narration:**
> [pivot] But here's the surprising part. God's law doesn't stop at “don't take.”
> [story] Farmers in Israel weren't allowed to harvest right up to the edges of their fields, or pick up every fallen grape.
> [grave] {{ESV Leviticus 19:10}}
> [talk] Christians have long read this as the other side of the eighth commandment. Holding on too tightly can be a kind of taking too.

## 5. Same city, opposite hands (2:55–3:40)

**Visual:** Back to the ochre Jericho sheet, now with a glowing sycamore tree. A small round figure (Zacchaeus) climbs it. Jesus is not shown: a warm light passes beneath the tree, and a torn strip arrives with the words. Then Zacchaeus's coins pour out of a bag toward small figures, a coral "×4" beside the stream. Final beat: two cut-out hands side by side, one closed (Achan), one open (Zacchaeus).
**On screen:** {{ESV Luke 19:8}} (keep Zacchaeus's words, from "Behold, Lord") · LUKE 19:8 ESV · label **SAME CITY. OPPOSITE HANDS.**
**Narration:**
> [pivot] Centuries later, Jesus walked into Jericho. The same city where Achan stole.
> [story] A tax collector lived there named Zacchaeus. He was rich, and everyone knew how he got that way.
> [story] He climbed a tree just to see Jesus. Jesus said he was coming to his house.
> [story] And Zacchaeus stood up and said this.
> [grave] {{ESV Luke 19:8}}
> [talk] Achan took and hid. Zacchaeus gave back four times over. [pause]
> [grave] Same city, but opposite hands.

## 6. Steal no longer (3:40–4:10)

**Visual:** A clean cream sheet, a carpenter's bench in paper cut-outs: a hammer, a saw, a loaf of bread. A torn verse strip lands across it; the highlighter sweeps "share with anyone in need". Then a pair of glowing hands opens, palms up, and small lights drift out of them to figures around the frame.
**On screen:** {{ESV Ephesians 4:28}} · EPHESIANS 4:28 ESV · label **NOT JUST "STOP." SHARE.**
**Narration:**
> [talk] The apostle Paul put it this way, writing to Christians in Ephesus.
> [grave] {{ESV Ephesians 4:28}}
> [talk] Notice where that sentence ends. Not with “stop taking,” but with “share.” [beat]
> [grave] The opposite of stealing isn't just keeping your hands to yourself. It's opening them.

## 7. What are your hands for (4:10–4:35)

**Visual:** Pull back across the wall: the map of shares, Jericho, the field with its edges, the open hands. The tablets return, the eighth line glowing. Black label: **HANDS THAT TOOK CAN LEARN TO GIVE.** Then the next tag slides in: the ninth line on the tablet warms.
**On screen:** Label **WHAT ARE YOUR HANDS FOR?**
**Narration:**
> [story] The God who gave every family its field is a giving God.
> [talk] So the real question isn't only “have I ever stolen?” It's “what are my hands for?” [pause]
> [grave] Hands that once took can learn to give. [hold]
> [talk] Next time, the ninth commandment: what happens when we steal someone's good name.

**On screen:** THE TEN COMMANDMENTS · PART 8, **THE EIGHTH COMMANDMENT**, **NEXT: THE NINTH COMMANDMENT**, then the ESV copyright notice.

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:15 is the eighth commandment (Catholic and Lutheran count it as the seventh).
- **Paraphrases (Leviticus).** The farmers' rule (no reaping to the edge, no gathering fallen grapes) paraphrases Leviticus 19:9–10a; only the second sentence of 19:10 is quoted.
- **Partial verses.** Joshua 7:21 (from "I coveted them"), Leviticus 19:10 (second sentence) and Luke 19:8 (Zacchaeus's words) are read in part; trimmed in the filled file with `scripts/trim_verses.py`.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** "Devoted to him ... nobody was to keep any of it" paraphrases Joshua 6:17–19. "A beautiful cloak, and some silver, and a bar of gold" paraphrases the first half of Joshua 7:21. The share of land for every family paraphrases Numbers 26:52–56 and Joshua 13–19. Zacchaeus climbing the tree and Jesus coming to his house is Luke 19:1–6 (Jericho named in 19:1). None are shown as verse cards.
- **Achan's end** (Joshua 7:24–26) and Israel's defeat at Ai are left out to keep the episode family-friendly and on the commandment.
- **Modern examples** in section 3 and the reading in section 4 ("the other side of the eighth commandment") are the narrator's, attributed as a long Christian reading (e.g. the Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 111, asks to work "so that I may share with those in need").
