# Jonah: full script

A prophet runs from God, is swallowed by a great fish, and gets angry when God forgives his enemies. The book of Jonah. A stand-alone story. Working title: *Should Not I Pity Nineveh?*

**Runtime:** about 3½ minutes (≈500 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as Esther and Daniel: the simple round PPL figures (one straight stubby arm each, dot eyes), the backdrop kit (banded skies, a harbour, a ship in a storm, the deep sea, the great walled city of Nineveh, a hillside with a leafy plant), foregrounds that belong to the place. The great fish is a big friendly-scary whale-like shape, never gory. Vertical 9:16 only.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (`python scripts/fill_esv.py --bg`, then trim by hand to the "keep only" text).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The book of Jonah isn't really about a big fish. It's about a God whose mercy reaches even the enemies we think don't deserve it, and a prophet who didn't want it to.

**Misconception it overturns:** that Jonah is a fish story with a moral about obeying. The fish is only chapter 2; the point is chapter 4. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 5 (Jonah's anger) and section 6 (God's question).

**Spine:** God tells Jonah to go to Nineveh, BUT Jonah runs the other way, THEREFORE God sends a storm. THEREFORE the sailors throw Jonah into the sea, BUT God sends a great fish, AND Jonah prays. THEREFORE Jonah goes to Nineveh, AND the whole city turns to God, THEREFORE God spares them. BUT Jonah is furious, because he knew God would be merciful. THEREFORE God asks him a question, and the book ends there.

**Re-hooks:** 0:25 ("Nineveh was the capital"), 0:50 ("But Jonah ran"), 1:30 ("Then a great fish"), 2:10 ("And the whole city listened"), 2:40 ("And Jonah was furious").

## 0. Cold open: the fish (0:00–0:25)

**Visual:** The deep sea, dark blue-green, shafts of light from above. A huge shadow glides past, then a great fish with its mouth open swims toward a tiny figure sinking in the water. Cut to black. One label at a time.
**On screen:** Label **JONAH**, then **NOT ABOUT A FISH**
**Narration:**
> [story] A man thrown into a stormy sea. Swallowed by a great fish. Three days and three nights inside it. [beat]
> [talk] That's the part everyone remembers.
> [pivot] But the fish isn't the point of the story. [beat]
> [talk] The point is what happened after.

**Transition:** Cut to Jonah at home in the hills of Israel.

## 1. Go to Nineveh (0:25–0:50)

**Visual:** Jonah (a youngish man with a short black beard and an orange tunic) at his house in the hills. A soft light and rings of light come down (God is never a figure). Then the map: a little strip shows Israel on one side and, far to the east, the great city of Nineveh with high walls, soldiers and chariots, a winged-bull gate.
**On screen:** {{ESV Jonah 1:2}} (keep only "Arise, go to Nineveh, that great city, and call out against it, for their evil has come up before me.") · JONAH 1:2 ESV · label **NINEVEH**
**Narration:**
> [story] The word of the LORD came to a prophet named Jonah.
> [grave] {{ESV Jonah 1:2}}
> [story] Nineveh was the capital of Assyria, the cruellest empire of its day, and Israel's enemy.

## 2. The other way (0:50–1:30)

**Visual:** A busy harbour at Joppa; Jonah buys a ticket and boards a ship sailing west, away from Nineveh (an arrow on a little map turning the opposite way). Then out at sea: the sky turns black, a huge storm, waves over the deck, sailors throwing cargo overboard and praying to their gods. Jonah asleep below deck. The sailors wake him; he points at himself. Then they lift him over the side, and the sea goes calm.
**On screen:** Label **THE OTHER WAY**
**Narration:**
> [pivot] But Jonah ran. He got on a ship going the opposite way, as far from Nineveh as he could get.
> [story] So God sent a great storm. The sailors were terrified, and every one of them prayed to his own god. Jonah was fast asleep.
> [story] Jonah told them the truth. I'm running from the LORD. Throw me into the sea, and it will be calm.
> [story] They didn't want to. But in the end they did. And the sea went still.

## 3. Inside the fish (1:30–2:00)

**Visual:** Jonah sinking through the water, seaweed round his head. The great fish swallows him (a gentle gulp, no gore). Inside: a dark cavern-like belly with a little glow; Jonah kneeling and praying. Then the fish spits him out onto a sunny beach.
**On screen:** {{ESV Jonah 2:9}} (keep only "Salvation belongs to the LORD!") · JONAH 2:9 ESV
**Narration:**
> [pivot] Then a great fish swallowed Jonah.
> [story] And there, inside the fish, Jonah prayed. He thanked God for saving his life.
> [grave] {{ESV Jonah 2:9}}
> [story] And the fish spat him out onto dry land.

## 4. Forty days (2:00–2:35)

**Visual:** Jonah walking into Nineveh's great gate between the winged bulls; the city huge and crowded. He stands on a step and calls out one sentence. People stop and listen. Then everyone, from the market sellers to the king on his throne, takes off fine clothes and puts on rough sackcloth; the king sits in ashes; even the cattle and sheep wear sackcloth. A soft light falls on the city.

**Narration:**
> [story] So God spoke to Jonah a second time. And this time, he went.
> [grave] {{ESV Jonah 3:4}}
> [pivot] And the whole city listened. From the king down, they stopped their violence and turned to God.
> [grave] {{ESV Jonah 3:10}}

## 5. Furious (2:35–3:10)

**Visual:** A hillside outside the city. Jonah sits alone, arms folded, scowling at Nineveh below. He shouts up at the sky. Then a leafy plant grows up over him and gives him shade; he smiles. Next morning a worm nibbles the stem, the plant withers, a hot east wind blows and the sun beats down; Jonah is miserable and angry again.
**On screen:** {{ESV Jonah 4:2}} (keep only "for I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful, slow to anger and abounding in steadfast love, and relenting from disaster.") · JONAH 4:2 ESV
**Narration:**
> [pivot] And Jonah was furious. Because this is exactly why he ran.
> [grave] {{ESV Jonah 4:2}}
> [story] Jonah sat on a hill to see what would happen. God made a plant grow to shade him, and Jonah was glad.
> [story] Then God sent a worm, and the plant died. And Jonah was angry about the plant.

## 6. The question (3:10–3:40)

**Visual:** God's voice as light and rings over the hill. Jonah looks down at the withered plant, then out at the great city: thousands of people in the streets, children playing, cattle in the fields at sunset. The camera slowly pulls back to show the whole city.
**On screen:** {{ESV Jonah 4:11}} (keep only "And should not I pity Nineveh, that great city, in which there are more than 120,000 persons who do not know their right hand from their left, and also much cattle?") · JONAH 4:11 ESV
**Narration:**
> [story] So God said to him, you cared about a plant you didn't even grow.
> [grave] {{ESV Jonah 4:11}}
> [talk] And that's where the book ends. With a question.

## 7. Our question (3:40–3:55)

**Visual:** Jonah alone on the hill at dusk, looking at the lit-up city. End card: the great city at night, a soft light over it.
**On screen:** Label **WHO IS TOO FAR?** · end card with ESV credit
**Narration:**
> [talk] Jonah never answers it. Maybe that's because the question is for us.
> [pivot] Is there anyone we think is too far gone for God's mercy?
