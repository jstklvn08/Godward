# The Ninth Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 9: the ninth commandment only. Exodus 20, Deuteronomy 19, 1 Kings 21, Mark 14, Ephesians 4. Working title: *No False Witness*.

**Runtime:** about 3.8 minutes (≈520 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as part 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as part 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (BibleGateway fill, then scripts/trim_verses.py).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The ninth commandment isn't only "don't lie"; it guards your neighbor's name and life from your words, and its opposite is truth spoken in love.

**Misconception it overturns:** that it is a general rule about honesty, aimed at me. It is aimed at what my words do to someone else ("against your neighbor"). Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 3, 5 and 6.

**Spine:** Israel's courts ran on witnesses, THEREFORE God required two or three. BUT two witnesses could be bought (Naboth), THEREFORE the commandment is about the neighbor: words take names, homes, lives. AND it happened to Jesus, THEREFORE false witness is no small sin. BUT the courtroom is now a group chat, THEREFORE we all stand as witnesses daily. THEREFORE God calls for truth, in love. AND Naboth's story began with wanting, which leads to the tenth.

**Re-hooks:** 0:45 ("But even two witnesses can be bought"), 1:45 ("And centuries later, it happened again"), 2:20 ("But the courtroom hasn't gone away"), 3:30 ("And remember how Naboth's story began").

## 5. The courtroom now (2:25–3:00)

**Visual:** The camera rises to a deep-blue sheet: a giant paper phone, chat bubbles stacking up, a rumour bubble passed from one round figure to the next, growing at each step. A screenshot is cropped by coral scissors. A paper "SHARE" arrow flies off the edge and can't be pulled back.
**On screen:** labels GROUP CHAT, SCREENSHOT, COMMENTS · "I'M JUST SAYING..." · HALF THE STORY · CAN'T TAKE IT BACK
**Narration:**
> [pivot] We don't stand at the city gate any more. But the courtroom hasn't gone away.
> [talk] Now it's a group chat. A screenshot. A comment under a video.
> [talk] A rumour about someone, passed on because it's interesting. [beat]
> [talk] “I'm just saying what I heard.” That's false witness, today.
> [story] Sometimes it's half the story, cropped to look worse. And once it's shared, you can't take it back.

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:16 is the ninth commandment. Catholic and Lutheran tradition count 20:16 as the eighth; the narration never says "ninth"; only the title and end card do.
- **Partial verses.** Deuteronomy 19:15 (the last sentence), 1 Kings 21:13 (the charge) and Ephesians 4:15 (up to "into Christ") are read and shown in part, via `scripts/trim_verses.py`.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** Trials at the city gate: Deuteronomy 21:19, 22:15, Ruth 4:1–11. Ahab, Naboth and "his family's land" ("the inheritance of my fathers"): 1 Kings 21:1–3. Jezebel's letters and the two worthless men accusing him in public ("in the presence of the people"): 1 Kings 21:8–13. Naboth's death and the vineyard taken: 1 Kings 21:13–16 (the stoning itself is not narrated, for a family audience). "Looking for witnesses" paraphrases Mark 14:55. "Stood silent": Mark 14:61. "The one who called himself the truth": John 14:6. None are shown as verse cards.
- **Interpretation.** "Guarding your neighbor's good name" is the long Christian reading (e.g. the Heidelberg and Westminster catechisms) and is attributed. The three questions in section 7 are the narrator's application.
- **Ahab and Jezebel** are shown as a gold-foil crown and sealed letters, not as figures; the two false witnesses are small grey figures without a glow, kept non-frightening.
- **Link to part 10.** Naboth's story starts with Ahab's wanting (1 Kings 21:2–4), which leads into the tenth commandment (coveting).
