# Genesis 1–11: How a Good World Went Wrong

Narration script for one narrator. It follows the story arc of BibleProject's "Book of Genesis Summary: A Complete Animated Overview (Part 1)" in original wording, with the New King James Version as the Bible reference.

The narration paraphrases scripture. Where a verse should appear on screen, the line **On screen** gives its reference, so the exact NKJV text can be added with the required credit (see the end of this file).

---

## 1. Order out of chaos

**References:** Genesis 1:1–25
**On screen:** Genesis 1:2

> The Bible's second sentence describes a dark, empty, shapeless deep. Then God goes to work. He speaks, and there is light. He divides, gathers and fills, until chaos has become a home, teeming with living things. And again and again, he calls it good.

## 2. A job description

**References:** Genesis 1:26–28; 2:7, 15, 18
**On screen:** Genesis 1:27

> Then he makes humans, and gives them a title nothing else receives. They are made in his image. That isn't about how they look. It's a job description. They're meant to show what God is like, and to look after his world on his behalf, so that it thrives. God blesses them and tells them to fill the earth. Watch for that word, blessing. Genesis keeps coming back to it. In Hebrew, the name for this new creature is adam, a word tied to adamah, the ground. God shapes a human from dust and breathes in life, and dust becomes a living being. He places the man in a garden, gives him a helper suited to him, and the work begins.

## 3. Two trees

**References:** Genesis 2:9, 16–17
**On screen:** Genesis 2:17

> But how will the work be done? Genesis answers with two trees. One is the tree of life, and it stays open to them. The other is the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, and that one is off limits. Until now, it has been God who says what is good. The prohibition leaves humans with a real decision. Will they rely on God to tell them what is good? Or will they claim that right for themselves? Walking away from God means walking away from where life comes from. So the warning is blunt. Eat from this tree, and you will die.

## 4. The snake's offer

**References:** Genesis 1:27; 3:1–5
**On screen:** Genesis 3:4–5

> Chapter three brings in a snake. Genesis tells us only two things about it. God made it, and it is more cunning than any other animal. We soon learn what it wants. It questions God's words, then flatly contradicts them. You won't die, it says. Eat this, and you'll be like God. And here is the painful part. They already were like God. They'd been made in his image from the start. The snake sold them something they already owned.

## 5. Everything comes apart

**References:** Genesis 3:6–13
**On screen:** Genesis 3:9

> They eat. And everything comes apart, very quickly. Two people who had been completely at ease with each other suddenly feel exposed, and they cover themselves. When God comes walking in the garden, they hide from him as well. When he asks what happened, the man blames the woman, and the woman blames the snake. In the space of a few verses, trust has failed in every direction.

## 6. A promise in the verdict

**References:** Genesis 3:14–24
**On screen:** Genesis 3:15

> Now the story pauses for God's verdict, and the first words go to the snake. It looks as though it has won. It hasn't. God says that one day the woman's own offspring, her Seed, will bruise the snake's head, and that the snake will bruise his heel. Someone is coming who will beat this enemy, and will be hurt in doing it. Stop and notice the timing. The humans have only just betrayed God, and the first thing he says about their future is a rescue. The rescue doesn't cancel the cost, though. Childbirth will be painful. Marriage will become a struggle for control. Work will be hard, the ground will resist, and at the end of it all, dust returns to dust. God clothes them with garments of skin, and sends them out of the garden.

## 7. Two brothers

**References:** Genesis 4:1–16
**On screen:** Genesis 4:7

> What follows is a story of damage spreading outward. Adam and Eve have two sons, Cain and Abel. God accepts Abel's offering and not Cain's, and Cain burns with resentment. God warns him that sin is lying at the door, and that he must master it. Cain goes out to the field and kills his brother anyway. The fracture that began in a marriage has now reached a family.

## 8. A song about revenge

**References:** Genesis 4:17–24

> Cain goes on to build a city. Several generations later comes Lamech, who takes two wives, then composes a poem bragging that he killed a man for wounding him. If Cain would be avenged sevenfold, he says, he will be avenged seventy-sevenfold. Violence has become something to sing about.

## 9. The strangest passage

**References:** Genesis 6:1–4, 11

> Chapter six opens with one of the strangest passages in the Bible. The sons of God take whichever women they choose, and the Nephilim appear, the mighty men of old. Some readers take these sons of God to be fallen spiritual beings. Others see kings who claimed divine descent. Either way, the result is the same. The powerful take what they want, and the earth fills with violence.

## 10. A grieving God

**References:** Genesis 6:5–8, 13–18; 9:1, 12–16
**On screen:** Genesis 6:6; Genesis 9:13

> And God's response isn't cold. Genesis says it grieved him in his heart that he had made humans. His creation is being destroyed by the very people he put in charge of it. So he sends a flood to wash the earth clean of its evil, and he saves one righteous man, Noah, with his family, in an ark. When the waters go down, it feels like starting over. God repeats the blessing he gave at the beginning, to be fruitful and fill the earth, and he sets his rainbow in the clouds as the sign of his covenant.

## 11. Here we go again

**References:** Genesis 9:20–25

> For a moment, the world has a second chance. Then Noah plants a vineyard, gets drunk, and lies uncovered in his tent, and his son Ham dishonors him. A man, something planted in the ground, nakedness and shame. We have been here before.

## 12. Bricks and a tower

**References:** Genesis 11:1–9
**On screen:** Genesis 11:4

> And it keeps going. At a place called Babel, people learn to make bricks, and with that new ability they set out to build a city, and a tower reaching into the heavens, to make a name for themselves. It's the same old impulse, now at the scale of a civilization. God confuses their language, and scatters them across the earth.

## 13. Our world

**References:** Genesis 3:15; 12:1–3
**On screen:** Genesis 12:3

> Put these stories together, and they keep saying one thing. Again and again, God gives people the chance to care for his world, and again and again, they wreck it. And Genesis doesn't treat that as someone else's problem. This is our world. A good world that we have damaged, because each of us, in our own way, has taken that decision about good and evil for ourselves. But the promise made at the start hasn't gone away. A descendant is still coming, one who will take on evil at its root, and be wounded in the fight. So what will God do now, with a scattered and broken world? The answer starts with an old man named Abram, and a promise that through him, all the families of the earth would be blessed.
