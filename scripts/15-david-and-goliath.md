# David and Goliath: full script

A shepherd boy faces a giant in the valley of Elah. 1 Samuel 16:1–13 and 17. It follows The Fall of Jericho (Israel in the land) a few hundred years on, when Israel has a king. Working title: *The Battle Is the LORD's*.

**Runtime:** about 4 minutes (≈600 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as The Ten Plagues, The Red Sea and Jericho: the simple round PPL figures (one straight stubby arm each, dot eyes, `kid:true` for David and the other children), the backdrop kit (banded skies, hills, olive trees, tents, foregrounds that belong to the place), SC animals (sheep, a lion, a bear). Vertical 9:16 only.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (`python scripts/fill_esv.py --bg`, then `scripts/trim_verses.py`).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** David and Goliath isn't really about being brave enough to beat your giants. It's about whose battle it is. David wins because he trusts the LORD, not his sling.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the story's lesson is "be like David, face your giants". Named in the cold open, paid off in section 6 ("the battle is the LORD's") and section 8.

**Spine:** Israel's army faces the Philistines across a valley, BUT the Philistines have a giant, THEREFORE every soldier is afraid, even King Saul. BUT God has already chosen someone nobody noticed, a shepherd boy, THEREFORE David comes to the camp with bread for his brothers. THEREFORE he hears the giant mock God, AND he offers to fight. BUT Saul's armour doesn't fit, THEREFORE David goes with a sling and five stones. BUT he says the battle is the LORD's, THEREFORE one stone brings the giant down, and the army that was afraid wins.

**Re-hooks:** 0:25 ("Every morning, for forty days"), 1:10 ("But God had already chosen someone"), 1:55 ("Then David heard the giant"), 2:40 ("Goliath laughed"), 3:20 ("So who's the hero?").
