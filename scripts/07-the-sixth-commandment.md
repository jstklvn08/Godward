# The Sixth Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 6: the sixth commandment only. Exodus 20, Genesis 4 and 9, Matthew 5, 1 John 3. Working title: *Your Brother's Keeper*.

**Runtime:** about 3.7 minutes (≈510 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as episode 1. Family-friendly: no violence shown; harm only as a symbol (a torn paper figure, a glow that dims).
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as episode 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV.

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** "You shall not murder" protects every life because every person bears God's image, and Jesus shows it starts in the heart: the anger we feed, and the neighbour we refuse to keep.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the sixth commandment is the "easy one" most people keep without trying. Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 6 and 7.

**Spine:** Cain is angry, THEREFORE God warns him that sin is crouching at the door. BUT Cain doesn't rule it, THEREFORE his brother's life is lost, and Abel's blood cries out to God. THEREFORE we ask why one life matters so much: God made us in his image. THEREFORE at Sinai God guards life: you shall not murder. BUT Jesus goes underneath it, to anger, THEREFORE nobody keeps it easily. THEREFORE the command becomes a call to love: we are our brother's keeper.

**Re-hooks:** 0:30 ("It started with a feeling"), 1:20 ("Then God came to Cain"), 1:45 ("Why does one life matter that much?"), 2:30 ("Then Jesus opened it up"), 3:10 ("But there's a flip side").

**On screen:** Black label: **THE "EASY" ONE?** · then {{ESV Exodus 20:13}} · EXODUS 20:13 ESV

> [talk] It is the first death in the Bible. And it didn't start with a weapon. It started with a feeling. [beat]

> [story] Both brought an offering to God. God accepted Abel's offering, but not Cain's.

> [story] So God warned him, the way you would warn a friend.

## 2. The field (1:00–1:20)

**Visual:** The field at dusk, wide. Cain's red glow and Abel's soft gold glow walk out together. The camera stays far away. A paper figure of Abel is torn in two (a clean paper tear, no blood), and his glow dims to nothing. Only the grass moves.
**On screen:** Label: **GENESIS 4:8**
**Narration:**
> [pivot] But Cain didn't rule over it.
> [story] He asked his brother to come out to the field. And there, Cain rose up against Abel and took his life.
> [talk] The Bible tells it in one short sentence. There are no details. Just a life that is gone.

## 3. Crying from the ground (1:20–1:45)

> [grave] {{ESV Genesis 4:10}}
> [talk] Nobody else saw it happen. But God did. Abel's life mattered to him, even when no one was left to speak for it.

## 5. The sixth word (2:10–2:30)

**Visual:** The mountain in fire and smoke, then the tablets close: the sixth line burns gold. A strip with "kill" crossed out, and "murder" above it.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 20:13}} · EXODUS 20:13 ESV · labels OLD: KILL / NEW: MURDER

> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 20:13}}

> [talk] Cain asked if he was his brother's keeper. The answer, all along, was yes.

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:13 is the sixth commandment (Catholic and Lutheran count it fifth).
- **Partial verses.** Genesis 4:7 (from "sin is crouching"), Genesis 4:10 (God's words from "The voice"), Genesis 9:6 (last clause, from "God made man") and Matthew 5:22 (to the first "liable to judgment") are trimmed with `scripts/trim_verses.py`.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** Genesis 4:3–5 (offerings, "his face fell"), Genesis 4:8 (the field), Genesis 4:9 (Cain's answer; "am I my brother's keeper?" is echoed in section 7 without quotation marks), Matthew 5:21 (he quoted the old command) and Matthew 5:23–24 (make peace first). None are shown as verse cards.
- **"Kill" and "murder".** The KJV reads "kill"; the Hebrew *ratsach* is generally understood as unlawful killing of a person, so the ESV and most modern versions read "murder". The script claims only that.
- **1 John 3:12** names Cain ("We should not be like Cain..."); the narration says only that the next verse names him.
- **Protecting life** as the positive side of the command is the long Christian reading (for example the Heidelberg Catechism, Q&A 105–107); attributed as "Christians have long read".
- **Family-friendly.** No weapon, no blood shown. Abel's death is a paper tear and a glow dimming, seen from far away.
