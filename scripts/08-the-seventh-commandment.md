# The Seventh Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 7: the seventh commandment only. Exodus 20, Genesis 2, Deuteronomy 7, Hosea 2, Matthew 5 and John 8. Working title: *A Promise Kept*.

**Runtime:** about 3.5 minutes (≈500 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as episode 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as episode 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (filled from BibleGateway's ESV text; see the series brief).
**Audience:** strictly family-friendly. The episode talks about promises and faithfulness. Pictures: rings, two cords tied in one knot, two hands, a home with its lights on. Nothing suggestive anywhere.

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The seventh commandment protects the promise of marriage, and behind it stands a God who keeps his promises, even to people who break theirs.

**Misconception it overturns:** that this commandment is only a grown-up rule about one wrong act. Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 5 and 7.

**Spine:** God joins two people with a promise to hold fast, THEREFORE the seventh commandment guards that promise like a wall around a home. BUT the promise mirrors something bigger: God is the faithful one, THEREFORE the prophets called Israel's idols a broken promise, and God still brought his people home. BUT Jesus says faithfulness starts in the heart, THEREFORE none of us can claim we kept it perfectly. BUT Jesus meets a promise breaker with grace and a new start, THEREFORE we keep our promises because God keeps his.

**Re-hooks:** 0:45 ("Hold fast"), 1:20 ("But here's the bigger picture"), 2:00 ("And his people didn't"), 2:40 ("So is there any hope").

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:14 is the seventh commandment.
- **Family-friendly.** The word "adultery" is defined only as "breaking the promise of marriage". Matthew 5:28 is not quoted; its point is paraphrased ("it begins in the heart"). Genesis 2:24 is read only up to "hold fast to his wife". The John 8 story names no detail beyond "she had broken this commandment".
- **Partial verses.** Genesis 2:24 (up to "hold fast to his wife"), Deuteronomy 7:9 (up to "steadfast love") and John 8:11 (Jesus' words only) are trimmed after filling with `scripts/trim_verses.py`.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** "Bring her home" summarises Hosea 3:1; the prophets' marriage picture is Hosea 1–3, Jeremiah 3 and Ezekiel 16; "the one without sin should throw the first stone" paraphrases John 8:7; "it begins in the heart" paraphrases Matthew 5:28. None is shown as a verse card.
- **Hosea.** "God asked the prophet Hosea to live out that picture" follows Hosea 1:2 and 3:1; the reading of it as God's faithfulness to Israel is attributed ("Christians have long read this").
- **John 7:53–8:11.** Many Bibles (the ESV included) mark this passage as not in the earliest manuscripts. The script tells it plainly, as most churches read it; mention it in the description if wanted.
- **Promise stars** in section 3 (rainbow, Abraham's stars, the sea) are illustrative of Genesis 9, Genesis 15 and Exodus 14.
