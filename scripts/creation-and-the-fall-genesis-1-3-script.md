# The Creation and the Fall: Genesis 1–3

Narration script for one narrator, based on the provided summary of Genesis 1–3.

---

## 1. In the beginning

**Reference:** Genesis 1:1–2

> In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. The earth was formless and empty, and darkness covered the deep. Then God spoke.

## 2. Six days

**Reference:** Genesis 1:3–25

> On the first day, there was light, separated from the darkness. On the second, the sky, dividing the waters. On the third, dry land, seas and plants. On the fourth, the sun, the moon and the stars. On the fifth, creatures filled the seas and birds filled the sky. And on the sixth, animals walked the land. But God wasn't finished.

## 3. In his image

**Reference:** Genesis 1:26–2:3

> The climax of creation was humanity. God made human beings, male and female, in his own image. He blessed them, and gave them charge over the earth. Then he looked at everything he had made, and it was “very good.” On the seventh day, God rested, and he made that day holy.

## 4. Dust and breath

**Reference:** Genesis 2:7–8, 15

> Genesis then moves in closer. The Lord God formed the man from the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils “the breath of life.” He placed him in a garden in Eden, to work it and take care of it.

## 5. Two trees

**Reference:** Genesis 2:9, 16–17

> In the middle of the garden stood two trees: the tree of life, and the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. The man could eat freely from any tree except the second one. If he ate from that tree, God warned, he would certainly die.

## 6. Not alone

**Reference:** Genesis 2:18–25

> Then God said something new. “It is not good for the man to be alone.” He brought the animals to the man to be named, but not one was a suitable helper. So God put the man into a deep sleep, took one of his ribs, and made a woman. When the man saw her, he said she was “bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh.” The two became one flesh, naked and without shame.

## 7. The serpent

**Reference:** Genesis 3:1–5

> Then came the serpent, craftier than any other wild animal. It asked the woman a question. “Did God really say...?” She explained God's command, adding that they must not even touch the fruit. And the serpent contradicted God directly. “You will not certainly die.” Eat it, the serpent claimed, and your eyes will be opened. You will be like God, knowing good and evil.

## 8. The fruit

**Reference:** Genesis 3:6–7

> The woman saw that the fruit looked good, and that it was desirable for gaining wisdom. She ate some. She gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate too. Their eyes were opened, just as promised. But all they discovered was their nakedness and their shame. So they sewed fig leaves together to cover themselves.

## 9. Where are you?

**Reference:** Genesis 3:8–13

> Then they heard the Lord God walking in the garden, in the cool of the day, and they hid among the trees. God called out. “Where are you?” The man admitted that he had hidden in fear. And then came the blame. He pointed to the woman, and to God for giving her to him. She pointed to the serpent.

## 10. The judgment

**Reference:** Genesis 3:14–19

> The serpent was cursed to crawl on its belly. God put hostility between it and the woman, and between their offspring. “He will crush your head, and you will strike his heel.” Christians have long read this as the first hint of a Savior. The woman would have greatly increased pain in childbearing, and conflict in her marriage. The man would work cursed ground through painful toil, among thorns and thistles, until death. “For dust you are and to dust you will return.”

## 11. Exile

**Reference:** Genesis 3:20–24

> Adam named his wife Eve, “because she would become the mother of all the living.” And in an act of mercy, God made garments of skin and clothed them. Then he banished them from Eden, so they would not eat from the tree of life and live forever. On the east side of the garden, he placed cherubim and a flaming sword, to guard the way back to that tree.
