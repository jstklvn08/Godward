# The Fourth Commandment: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 4: the fourth commandment only. Exodus 20:8–11, Genesis 2, Deuteronomy 5, Mark 2, Matthew 11. Working title: *A Day to Stop*.

**Runtime:** about 3.75 minutes (≈510 words at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style (videos/creation-and-the-fall), same as part 1.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice, same as part 1 (`voice/reference/own-voice/`, default modes), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (BibleGateway fill, then scripts/trim_verses.py).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The Sabbath began not as a burden but as a gift: a weekly stop, rooted in God's own rest at creation and in his rescue of slaves, that Jesus says was made for people and that points to the rest he offers.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the fourth commandment is a list of things you can't do on Sunday. Named in the cold open, paid off in sections 4 and 6.

**Spine:** Israel had been slaves who never rested, THEREFORE God commands a stop for everyone, even servants and animals. The reason is creation: God rested, THEREFORE people may step into a finished work. BUT Deuteronomy adds a second reason, rescue from slavery, THEREFORE the day is a weekly declaration of freedom. BUT the gift gets buried under rules, THEREFORE Jesus says it was made for man. THEREFORE Christians, who differ on how to keep it, agree that people were made to stop, and Jesus offers the rest it pointed to.

**Re-hooks:** 0:40 ("Look who's on that list"), 1:05 ("And why the seventh day?"), 1:50 ("Forty years later"), 2:20 ("But over time, the gift got buried").

## 0. Cold open: a boss who says stop (0:00–0:25)

**Visual:** A charcoal sheet. Two stone tablets stand in the dark, ten carved lines glowing faintly; the fourth line lights up. Beside them a glowing round worker figure with a pickaxe, and a seven-square week strip; six squares fill, the seventh glows gold. Coral marker circles the seventh.
**On screen:** Black label: **A COMMAND TO STOP?** · then {{ESV Exodus 20:8}} · EXODUS 20:8 ESV
**Narration:**
> [story] Imagine a boss who orders you to stop working.
> [talk] Not for a holiday. One whole day, every single week, for the rest of your life.
> [pivot] That's the fourth commandment.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 20:8}}
> [talk] Most people hear it as a list of things you can't do on Sunday. [beat]
> [pivot] But it started as a gift.

**Transition:** The camera slides down to a red sheet: Egypt.

## 1. Nobody rested (0:25–1:05)

**Visual:** EGYPT: brick-making figures bent under a red sky, a brick stack growing, label **NO DAY OFF**. Then a warm sheet: the household cast in a row (parents, son, daughter, two servants, an ox and a donkey, a stranger with a staff), each lighting up as named; a coral bracket joins them all.
**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 20:9-10}} · EXODUS 20:9-10 ESV · labels SONS & DAUGHTERS, SERVANTS, ANIMALS, SOJOURNER
**Narration:**
> [story] Israel had just come out of Egypt, where nobody ever rested. Bricks today, more bricks tomorrow, and no end in sight.
> [story] Now God gives them a new rhythm, with a full stop built in.
> [grave] {{ESV Exodus 20:9-10}}
> [talk] Look who's on that list. Sons, daughters and servants, even the animals and the foreigner.
> [talk] Nobody is left working so that somebody else can rest.

## 2. The seventh day (1:05–1:50)

**Visual:** A deep-blue cosmic sheet: seven torn paper circles in an arc, numbered one to six with small icons (light, sky, land, sun and moon, fish and birds, animals and people); the seventh circle is empty and glows soft gold. A torn verse strip lands below.
**On screen:** {{ESV Genesis 2:3}} · GENESIS 2:3 ESV · label **FINISHED, NOT TIRED**
**Narration:**
> [pivot] And why the seventh day?
> [story] In six days God made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and everything in them. And on the seventh day, he rested.
> [grave] {{ESV Genesis 2:3}}
> [talk] God wasn't tired. Many readers take it as the rest of a finished work, not the rest of exhaustion.
> [story] The Sabbath invites people to stop, and say: the world doesn't hang on me.

## 3. You were a slave (1:50–2:20)

**Visual:** Back to the red Egypt sheet, now torn open: the brick stack collapses, a broken chain in gold foil, the crowd walking out into light. Label **REMEMBER: YOU WERE A SLAVE**.
**On screen:** {{ESV Deuteronomy 5:15}} · DEUTERONOMY 5:15 ESV
**Narration:**
> [pivot] Forty years later, Moses repeats the commandment, with a second reason.
> [grave] {{ESV Deuteronomy 5:15}}
> [talk] Slaves don't get a day off. Their worth is how much they produce.
> [story] So every Sabbath said it again: you are not a slave any more.

## 4. Made for man (2:20–2:55)

**Visual:** A sand-and-ochre sheet: a wheat field, small glowing disciples walking through it, one plucking grain. Then torn paper rule slips pile up over the field (DON'T CARRY, DON'T WALK FAR, NO WORK), and a warm light strip with Jesus' words slides in on top (Jesus not shown; words on paper in light). The rule slips blow away.
**On screen:** {{ESV Mark 2:27}} (keep Jesus' words) · MARK 2:27 ESV
**Narration:**
> [pivot] But over time, the gift got buried.
> [story] By the time of Jesus, some teachers had piled careful rules around the day.
> [story] One Sabbath, his hungry disciples picked some grain in a field, and critics called it work.
> [talk] Jesus answered with a sentence that turns it right side up.
> [grave] {{ESV Mark 2:27}}
> [talk] It was made to serve people, not to crush them.

## 5. Christians differ (2:55–3:20)

**Visual:** A cream sheet split into three panels, each a small calendar page with a glowing day: SATURDAY, SUNDAY, and EVERY DAY (a soft light over a whole week). A coral underline joins them: **MADE TO STOP**.
**On screen:** labels SATURDAY · SUNDAY · REST IN CHRIST
**Narration:**
> [talk] So how do Christians keep it today? Honestly, they disagree.
> [talk] Some, like Seventh-day Adventists, keep Saturday, the day Jewish families still keep.
> [talk] Many churches rest and worship on Sunday, the day Jesus rose.
> [talk] Others believe Jesus fulfilled it, so no one day binds, but rest still matters.
> [story] But almost all of them agree that you were made to stop.

## 6. Rest offered (3:20–3:50)

**Visual:** The calendars fall away. A warm light sheet: a torn strip with Jesus' words in the light. The tired worker from the cold open sets down the pickaxe; his glow brightens. Final image: the tablets again, the fourth line glowing gold.
**On screen:** {{ESV Matthew 11:28}} · MATTHEW 11:28 ESV
**Narration:**
> [pivot] And Jesus went further still.
> [talk] He offered the rest it pointed to.
> [grave] {{ESV Matthew 11:28}}
> [talk] Christians have long read this as the deepest Sabbath of all. Not just a day off, but rest for the soul.
> [grave] You don't have to earn your worth.
> [grave] You can stop, because God never does. [pause]
> [story] Next time, the fifth commandment, and the people you came from. [hold]

**Visual:** Pull back to the whole wall: Egypt, the field, the seventh circle, the tablets. Sheets dim to black.

## Notes for production

- **Numbering.** Reformed/Protestant count, as in part 1: Exodus 20:8–11 is the fourth commandment (Catholic and Lutheran count it as the third).
- **Partial verse.** Mark 2:27 is trimmed to Jesus' words ("The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath."). Other verses are read whole.
- **Paraphrases, not quoted.** "In six days God made the heavens and the earth, the sea, and everything in them" paraphrases Exodus 20:11; the grain field paraphrases Mark 2:23–24; "forty years later" refers to Deuteronomy's setting at the end of the wilderness years. "Careful rules" refers to the teachers' traditions around the Sabbath (cf. Mark 7, Acts 1:12 "a Sabbath day's journey"); the rule slips on screen are illustrative.
- **"God never does" stop working.** Echoes John 5:17 ("My Father is working until now"); presented as narration, not quoted. "Rest of a finished work" is attributed ("many readers").
- **Views today.** Saturday (Jewish practice; Seventh-day Adventists and Seventh Day Baptists), Sunday as the Lord's Day (many Reformed, Catholic, Orthodox and other churches), and fulfilment in Christ (many Lutheran, Baptist and other readers, cf. Colossians 2:16–17, Hebrews 4:9–10). The script names the positions without ruling between them.
