# The Good Samaritan: full script

A lawyer asks Jesus "Who is my neighbour?", and Jesus answers with a story about a man beaten on the road, two religious men who pass by, and the last person anyone expected to stop. Luke 10:25–37. Working title: *Who Is My Neighbour?*

**Runtime:** about 2½ minutes (≈380 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as the Prodigal Son: the simple round PPL figures (one straight stubby arm each, dot eyes). The traveller (a plain tan tunic), the priest (white robe with a blue sash), the Levite (a grey-blue robe), the Samaritan (a deep red-and-gold striped robe, a donkey). Backdrop kit: the steep rocky road down from Jerusalem to Jericho, an inn at dusk. Vertical 9:16 only.
**Restraint:** the robbers are only long shadows and a scuffle of dust; the traveller is shown lying by the road with a torn tunic, no wounds. The bandages are white cloth.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (`python scripts/fill_esv.py --bg`, then trim by hand to the "keep only" text).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** The lawyer asked "Who is my neighbour?", meaning "Who do I have to love?" Jesus turns the question round: not who counts as my neighbour, but who will I be a neighbour to. And he makes the hero the person his listeners despised.

**Misconception it overturns:** that "Samaritan" just means a nice person. To Jesus's listeners, Samaritans were the enemy. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 4 (the Samaritan stops) and section 6 (go and do likewise).

**Spine:** A lawyer asks how to inherit eternal life, AND says love God and your neighbour, BUT asks who his neighbour is, THEREFORE Jesus tells a story. A man is robbed and left half dead. A priest passes by, AND a Levite passes by, BUT a Samaritan stops, AND bandages him, AND pays for him, THEREFORE Jesus asks which one was a neighbour, AND says go and do likewise.

**Re-hooks:** 0:20 ("A lawyer stood up"), 0:50 ("A man was going down"), 1:15 ("But a Samaritan"), 1:50 ("Which of these three"), 2:10 ("Go, and do likewise").
