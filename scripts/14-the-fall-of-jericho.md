# The Fall of Jericho: full script

Israel crosses the Jordan into the promised land and takes Jericho by walking round it, not by fighting. Joshua 2, 3, 5:13–6:27, with Hebrews 11:30 and Matthew 1:5. It follows the Ten Commandments (the people who heard God at Sinai) forty years on. Working title: *The Walls Came Down*.

**Runtime:** about 4 minutes (≈580 words with the scripture at 140–150 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** glowing-collage style, the same kit as The Ten Plagues and The Red Sea: the simple round PPL figures (one straight stubby arm each, dot eyes, `kid:true` for children), the backdrop kit (banded skies, palms, mud-brick walls and houses, foregrounds that belong to the place), SC animals. Vertical 9:16 only.
**Voice:** the narrator's own voice (`voice/reference/own-voice/`), natural edit.
**Translation:** ESV (`python scripts/fill_esv.py --bg`, then `scripts/trim_verses.py`).

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** Jericho didn't fall because Israel was strong. It fell because they trusted God enough to do something that looked foolish, and a pagan woman named Rahab trusted him too.

**Misconception it overturns:** that Jericho is a war story about a great army. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 5 ("Not a single sword") and section 7 (Hebrews: "By faith").

**Spine:** Israel finally reaches the promised land, BUT in the way stands Jericho, a city with walls no army could climb. THEREFORE Joshua sends spies, AND Rahab hides them, because she has heard what God did at the sea. THEREFORE the spies promise to save her family: a red cord in her window. BUT God's battle plan is strange: walk round the city, once a day, in silence, THEREFORE the people obey for six days. THEREFORE on the seventh day they walk seven times, the trumpets blow, the people shout, and the walls fall. BUT Rahab's house, marked by the red cord, is spared, THEREFORE she becomes part of Israel, and an ancestor of Jesus.

**Re-hooks:** 0:30 ("But first, the spies"), 1:20 ("Then God gave Joshua the strangest battle plan in the Bible"), 2:10 ("Day after day"), 2:55 ("On the seventh day"), 3:30 ("But one house was still standing").

> [story] Moses had died. Their new leader was Joshua.

> [pivot] But first, the spies.

A house built into the city wall, a lamp in the window; Rahab (a woman with a headscarf) lets them in and hides them on her flat roof under stalks of flax laid out to dry. The king's soldiers knock below; she points them away toward the hills. Then on the roof, she speaks to the spies quietly.

**Visual:** Rahab lowers the spies down the outside of the wall on a rope from her window. Before they go, they tie a bright scarlet cord in the window. Close on the red cord against the mud-brick wall, catching the moonlight.

**Visual:** The march, day after day, the camera holding still while the light changes: dawn, noon, dusk. The silent line of people, the priests with ram's horns, the ark carried on poles. On the walls, soldiers watch, then point and laugh, then grow uneasy. A child in the line holds her father's hand and stays quiet. Day numerals tick: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6.
**On screen:** Label ticks **DAY 1 … DAY 6** (one label at a time)
**Narration:**

## 6. The seventh day (3:05–3:35)

A long trumpet blast drawn as glowing rings; the whole crowd shouts, mouths open, arms up. Then the walls crack, lean and fall outward in a cloud of dust. Big focus beat.
