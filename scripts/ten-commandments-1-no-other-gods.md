# No Other Gods: full script

The Ten Commandments, part 1: the first commandment. Exodus 19–20 and 32–34. Working title: *The Commandment Israel Broke in Forty Days*.

**Runtime:** about 8 minutes (≈1,050 words including the scripture readings, at 140–145 words a minute, plus pauses).
**Look:** the glowing-collage style of `videos/creation-and-the-fall/6-vox-kurzgesagt-mix.mp4`, the same as episode 1.
**Voice:** one narrator, calm and curious, with short pauses before the turns.
**Translation:** English Standard Version (ESV). See "Scripture text" below.

## Thesis and spine

**Thesis:** Israel broke the first commandment not by dropping God, but by putting something they made in his place. God's answer was to write the same words again.

**Misconception it overturns:** that the golden calf was a simple swap of God for an idol. Named in the cold open, paid off in section 6.

**Spine:** God rescues Israel, THEREFORE he gives them the commandments, starting with who he is. BUT Moses vanishes for forty days, THEREFORE the people demand gods they can see. BUT they call the calf's feast "to the LORD", THEREFORE the first commandment is already broken, THEREFORE Moses breaks the tablets. BUT Moses pleads for them, THEREFORE God writes the words again.

**Re-hooks:** 1:35 ("Here's the strange thing"), 3:45 ("So how do people who heard God's voice…"), 4:50 ("Wait. A feast to the LORD?"), 5:40 ("The story could end there").

## Style rules for every scene

Same as `01-creation-and-the-fall.md`, with these additions:

- **The wall.** The story runs along one wall of taped paper sheets. Sinai sheets are charcoal and deep indigo; camp sheets are sand and ochre. The camera slides sideways between sheets, with torn edges and a dark gap showing in the pan.
- **Characters.** Moses, Aaron and the people are round, simple figures with dot eyes, white sticker borders, a soft halftone shade and a faint aura. Moses carries a staff. The crowd is a repeating cut-out pattern with a few faces picked out.
- **God.** Never shown as a person. God's presence is cloud, fire, lightning and light on the mountaintop, and God's speech appears as words on paper inside that light.
- **The tablets of stone.** Two rounded grey slabs with glowing carved lines. The text on them is never legible; quotations appear on torn strips instead.
- **The calf.** A gold-foil cut-out with a hard metallic shine. It is the only object in the film with no glow of its own: it reflects light but gives none.
- **Scripture.** Quotes appear on torn cream strips with tape, in serif type, with a yellow highlighter sweeping across the key words. The reference sits in a black label box with bold white sans type, followed by "ESV".
- **Annotations.** Coral marker circles and arrows, black labels and neon lines.
- **Pause markers.** On their own narration line: `[beat]` 0.3 s, `[pause]` 0.8 s, `[hold]` 1.5 s. They are timing notes for the edit and are never spoken.

Scripture is quoted exactly from the ESV. Everything else is paraphrase and is never presented as scripture.

## 0. Cold open: the broken stone (0:00–0:30)

**Visual:** A sand-coloured sheet. Two stone tablets lie shattered at the foot of a dark mountain, their carved lines still faintly glowing in the cracks. In the background, a gold calf on a pedestal catches the light. The camera holds, then a coral marker circles the broken pieces.

**On screen:** Black label: **WHY DID MOSES BREAK THE TEN COMMANDMENTS?**

**Narration:**
> These are the Ten Commandments. Or they were. Written in stone, carried down a mountain, and smashed before anyone could read them. Moses broke them himself. And the reason starts with the very first commandment, which Israel managed to break in about forty days.

> And it's not what most people picture. Israel didn't trade God in for an idol. What they did was quieter than that, and a lot more familiar.

**Transition:** The camera whips left along the wall to a sheet of deep blue: the sea, parted.

## 1. Freed first (0:30–1:05)

**Visual:** A quick three-sheet pan. Sheet one: brick-making figures bent under a red sky, labelled EGYPT. Sheet two: two walls of neon water with a path between them. Sheet three: the people on the far shore, small and glowing, looking back.

**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 14:30}} (keep the first clause, up to "the Egyptians") · EXODUS 14:30 ESV

**Narration:**
> To understand the commandments, you have to know who heard them first. Not a nation. Not yet. A crowd of slaves who'd just walked out of Egypt through the middle of the sea.

> {{ESV Exodus 14:30}} (read the first clause only)

> They didn't earn it. They didn't vote for it. They were rescued, and three months later, God brings them to a mountain in the desert called Sinai.

## 2. The mountain (1:05–1:35)

**Visual:** A tall charcoal sheet. The mountain fills the frame, wrapped in a thick cloud. Lightning in neon lines, fire glowing from the summit, smoke drifting up like a furnace. A neon boundary line is drawn around the foot of the mountain. The people stand behind it, their auras flickering.

**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 19:18}} · EXODUS 19:18 ESV

**Narration:**
> What happens next is one of the most dramatic scenes in the Bible. Thunder. Lightning. A trumpet growing louder and louder.

> {{ESV Exodus 19:18}}

> The people are told to stay behind a line. And then, out of the fire, God speaks.

## 3. Not a command (1:35–2:15)

**Visual:** Inside the light on the summit, one torn strip arrives on its own. The highlighter sweeps the words about bringing them out of Egypt. Coral marker underlines them and writes beside them: **NOT A RULE.** Then a second black label appears: **WHO I AM. WHAT I DID.**

**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 20:2}} · EXODUS 20:2 ESV

**Narration:**
> Here's the strange thing. The Ten Commandments don't begin with a commandment. They begin with this:

> {{ESV Exodus 20:2}}

> Before God tells them anything to do, he tells them who he is, and what he's already done. The rescue comes first. The rules come second.

> That order matters. These aren't instructions for earning God's favour. They're for people who already have it. A way of life for people who aren't slaves any more.

## 4. The first commandment (2:15–3:15)

**Visual:** The next strip slams down beneath the first. The highlighter sweeps "no other gods". Then the camera pulls back: around the strip, faint cut-outs of the gods of Egypt are pinned to the paper, a falcon-headed figure, a sun disc, a sacred bull, each with a coral X drawn through it. A small neon sun-disc labelled RA, a bull labelled APIS.

**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 20:3}} · EXODUS 20:3 ESV

**Narration:**
> And then the first command:

> {{ESV Exodus 20:3}}

> For people who'd grown up in Egypt, this was radical. Egypt had gods for everything. The sun, the river, the harvest, the king. You'd spread your trust around, and hope one of them came through.

> But the God who just beat Egypt says: not here. Not any more. You don't divide your loyalty. You don't keep a backup.

> And notice the little word "before". In the Hebrew, it's built on the word for "face": no other gods in front of my face. It's not only about who comes first on a list. It's about who else is in the room.

## 5. Forty days (3:15–3:50)

**Visual:** The camera slides down the mountain to the camp. Moses climbs up into the cloud and disappears. A black label counter in the corner ticks up: DAY 1… DAY 12… DAY 27… DAY 40. The people below look up at the mountain, then at each other. Their auras dim with each count.

**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 24:18}} (keep the last clause, about forty days and forty nights) · EXODUS 24:18 ESV

**Narration:**
> The people say yes. All of it. Everything the LORD has said, they will do. And Moses goes back up the mountain to receive the commandments in stone, and he's there for forty days and forty nights.

> Forty days is a long time to stare at a cloud. Down in the camp, the waiting starts to turn into something else.

> So how do people who heard God's voice out of the fire break his very first command before Moses even gets back?

## 6. The calf (3:50–5:15)

**Visual:** A sand-and-ochre sheet. The crowd gathers around Aaron. Gold earrings are pulled off and dropped into a pile, each one a small foil shape. A fire, a mould, and then the calf rises out of it, hard and shiny. Two torn strips come in one under the other, and coral marker circles the words about bringing them out of Egypt in both, joining them with an arrow.

**On screen:**
- {{ESV Exodus 32:1}} (keep the people's words to Aaron, up to "go before us") · EXODUS 32:1 ESV
- {{ESV Exodus 20:2}} · EXODUS 20:2 ESV
- {{ESV Exodus 32:4}} (keep the people's words about the calf) · EXODUS 32:4 ESV

**Narration:**
> Moses has vanished, the people say, and nobody knows what's become of him. So they go to Aaron with a demand: make us gods to go before us.

> So Aaron takes their gold, the gold they carried out of Egypt, and makes a calf.

> Now put two sentences side by side. On the mountain, God said:

> {{ESV Exodus 20:2}} (read up to "the land of Egypt")

> In the camp, the people point at the calf and say:

> {{ESV Exodus 32:4}} (read the people's words only)

> They haven't forgotten the rescue. They've given the credit to something they made.

> And here's the twist. Aaron announces a feast to the LORD.

> Wait. A feast to the LORD? Then what's the problem?

> [beat]

> That's exactly the problem. Remember: they didn't trade God in. They don't think they're leaving God. They think they're adding something. Something they can see, something that stays put. A god that goes before them on their terms.

> And that's exactly what the first commandment ruled out.

## 7. The breaking (5:15–5:40)

**Visual:** The cold-open sheet, now in motion. Moses comes down the mountain with the two glowing tablets. He sees the calf and the dancing. In held poses, he lifts the tablets and throws them. On impact, the paper tears along a jagged line, the carved glow scatters into sparks, and the calf's shine dulls to grey.

**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 32:19}} (keep the last clause, about throwing and breaking the tablets) · EXODUS 32:19 ESV

**Narration:**
> Moses comes down carrying the tablets, the writing of God carved into stone. He sees the calf. He sees the dancing. And he throws the tablets down and breaks them at the foot of the mountain.

> The covenant was broken before it was even delivered. So Moses breaks the stone to match.

> [pause]

## 8. The man in the gap (5:40–6:10)

**Visual:** Moses alone on a charcoal sheet, small against the mountain, facing the light. Behind him, the camp below, dim. A single strip arrives. Coral marker circles the words "blot me".

**On screen:** {{ESV Exodus 32:32}} · EXODUS 32:32 ESV

**Narration:**
> The story could end there. It doesn't. Moses climbs back up the mountain and pleads for the people. And he offers something astonishing:

> {{ESV Exodus 32:32}}

> [hold]

> Forgive them. And if you won't, take me instead.

## 9. Written again (6:10–6:55)

**Visual:** Two new, blank stone tablets carried up the mountain. The cloud descends and light passes in front of Moses, who bows. The carved lines glow into the new stone, the same pattern as the broken ones. Then Moses comes down and his face is glowing; the people step back from the light.

**On screen:**
- {{ESV Exodus 34:1}} · EXODUS 34:1 ESV
- {{ESV Exodus 34:6}} (keep from "The LORD, the LORD" to the end of the verse) · EXODUS 34:6 ESV

**Narration:**
> And God answers. Cut two new tablets, he tells Moses, and I will write on them the same words that were on the first ones, the ones you broke.

> Same words. Second chance. And this time, God tells Moses who he is:

> {{ESV Exodus 34:6}} (read from "The LORD, the LORD")

> God calls himself jealous in this same chapter. Not petty. Jealous the way a husband or wife is, when someone else is given what belongs to the marriage. The first commandment isn't God guarding his ego. It's God guarding a relationship.

## 10. Our calves and the answer (6:55–7:55)

**Visual:** The camera pulls back from the calf, and around it the paper fills with modern gold-foil cut-outs: a stack of coins, a phone, a trophy, a crown. None of them glows. A torn strip lands across the bottom.

**On screen:** {{ESV Psalm 115:8}} · PSALM 115:8 ESV

**Narration:**
> We don't usually melt down earrings any more. But the pull is the same: to take the good things we've been given and hand them the credit. To trust something we can hold, control, and see.

> A psalm says it bluntly:

> {{ESV Psalm 115:8}}

> You become like what you trust in. The first commandment isn't a fence around God. It's a door out of that trap.

> [pause]

> So why did Moses break the Ten Commandments? Because Israel had already broken the first one. Not by forgetting God, but by handing his place to something they could hold. And God's answer wasn't to give up on them. It was to write the same words again.

**On screen:** Series title card, then **NEXT: THE SECOND COMMANDMENT**. Then the ESV copyright notice (see below).

## Notes for production

- **Translation and permission.** All scripture is from the ESV, which is under copyright to Crossway. Crossway allows a limited number of ESV verses to be quoted without written permission if the copyright notice is shown, but check its current terms at esv.org for video use, especially once the channel is monetised. `fill_esv.py` adds the notice to the filled script; it also needs to go on screen (the end card is fine) and in the video description. The ESV API has its own terms, including a non-commercial use condition, so read those before using it for a monetised channel.
- **Verse references for narration-only paraphrases.** "Everything the LORD has said, they will do" paraphrases Exodus 24:3. "A feast to the LORD" is Exodus 32:5. "The writing of God" is Exodus 32:16. "Jealous" is Exodus 34:14. These are paraphrased, not quoted, so they need no ESV text.
- **Partial verses.** Where a scene note says to keep or read part of a verse, trim the filled text and keep the reference on screen. Check after filling that the kept part still reads as a complete phrase.
- **"Before" and "face".** Exodus 20:3 reads *'al-panay* in Hebrew, built on *panim*, "face". Translations render it "before me" or "besides me". Exodus 32:1's "go before us" is a different phrase (*lefanenu*) built on the same word. The script only claims the shared root, not that the phrases are identical. Confirm after filling that the ESV text of both verses uses "before".
- **The side-by-side in section 6.** It depends on Exodus 20:2 and 32:4 both saying the people were brought up out of the land of Egypt. Check the filled ESV wording keeps that parallel visible; if the wording differs, highlight the shared phrase only.
- **Timing of the calf.** Exodus 24:18 gives forty days on the mountain, and Exodus 32:1 says the people saw that Moses delayed. "About forty days" in the cold open is an approximation, not a quoted figure.
- **Egypt's gold and gods.** "The gold they carried out of Egypt" follows Exodus 12:35–36. The Egyptian gods on screen (Ra, Apis) are illustrative; Exodus 12:12 speaks of judgement on all the gods of Egypt without naming them.
- **Interpretive claims.** "Jealous the way a husband or wife is" reflects the marriage imagery the prophets use for the covenant (for example Hosea 2 and Jeremiah 3). The script presents it as an explanation, not as Exodus's own words. Section 10's modern application is the narrator's.
- **Commandment numbering.** Jewish tradition counts Exodus 20:2 as the first "word" and 20:3–6 as the second; Catholic and Lutheran tradition joins 20:3–6 as the first; Reformed and Orthodox tradition treats 20:3 as the first and 20:4–6 (images) as the second. This script follows the last of these, so "the second commandment" in the end card is the ban on carved images. If the series wants to stay neutral, change the end card to **NEXT: NO CARVED IMAGE**.
- **What's left out.** Exodus 32:25–28 (the Levites and the three thousand) and 32:35 (the plague) are omitted for runtime. If the series wants the full account, they fit between sections 7 and 8.
