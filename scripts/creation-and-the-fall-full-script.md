# Creation and the Fall: full script

Genesis 1–3, told by one narrator, in the visual style of `videos/creation-and-the-fall/6-vox-kurzgesagt-mix.mp4`.

Curly quotation marks in this file are used only for exact King James text.

## Visual rules (taken from clip 6)

| Element | Treatment |
|---|---|
| **Backgrounds** | Deep saturated color with paper grain, drifting stars and glowing particles |
| **Scripture** | Torn cream paper strips with tape, Georgia type, yellow highlighter sweeping across key words |
| **Labels** | Black boxes with bold white capitals, plus a black reference tag for each verse |
| **Annotations** | Yellow hand-drawn arrows, red marker circles and dotted connecting lines |
| **Characters** | Chubby and rounded with dot eyes, halftone shading, a white sticker border and a soft colored aura |
| **Motion** | Cut-outs jitter at 12 frames per second, while glow and particles stay smooth |
| **Transitions** | The camera slides sideways along one continuous paper wall |

**Color arc:** black and indigo (nothing, then space) → teal and violet (Eden) → crimson (temptation) → deep purple night (judgment), with one warm gold light that stays alive from Scene 16 to the end.

**Content rules**
- God is never drawn. He appears only as light, and his words appear as glowing text.
- The serpent is only ever labeled THE SERPENT, because Genesis 3 doesn't name who is behind it.
- She is labeled THE WOMAN until Scene 16, because Genesis doesn't call her Eve until 3:20.
- The characters wear simple clothing throughout. In Scene 13, shame is shown by their auras going out and leaf cut-outs being taped over them.

---

## Scene 1: The burning gate

**Palette:** deep purple night, in the style of clip 6's final scene.
**Visual:** Open close on the turning flaming sword. Pull back to reveal the arch and wings of light, and tag them with black labels. Embers drift upward. Beyond the arch, a faint glow of teal. A red marker circles the gate and draws a question mark. The camera then slides left, and the wall goes black.

> A sword made of fire, turning every way. Behind it, a garden that no human being can enter. So how did the best place on earth end up with a guard at the gate? To find out, we have to go back. Before there was a gate. Before there was a garden. Before there was anything at all.

## Scene 2: Without form

**Palette:** near-black paper, with one faint moving light.
**Visual:** The first paper strip is taped to the dark wall and highlighted word by word. Below it, black water moves slowly. A single soft light glides over its surface.
**Strips:** “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.” (Genesis 1:1) · “And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.” (Genesis 1:2)

> The Bible opens with one sentence. “In the beginning God created the heaven and the earth.” There's no argument for why God exists, and no account of where he came from. He is simply there, first. And the earth, at this point, isn't much to look at. “Without form, and void.” Dark water. No land, no life, no light. But something is moving. “And the Spirit of God moved upon the face of the waters.”

## Scene 3: Let there be light

**Palette:** an explosion of white and cyan into indigo.
**Visual:** The words appear in glowing type, one at a time. On the last word, the screen floods with light and particles burst outward across the paper. No sun or star is visible. A black label reads DAY 1. A small taped note reads NO SUN YET, with a red arrow pointing to a blank circle.
**Strip:** “And God said, Let there be light: and there was light.” (Genesis 1:3)

> Then God speaks. “Let there be light: and there was light.” Notice what's missing. There's no struggle, and no helper. God says it, and it is so. And here's something easy to miss. This light comes before the sun. The sun doesn't appear until day four.

## Scene 4: Six days

**Palette:** indigo warming into teal as the world fills.
**Visual:** A long paper timeline slides past with six stations. Each day pops on as a glowing cut-out: sky over water, land bursting with plants, sun and moon and stars, fish and birds, land animals. At the end, red marker lines connect Day 1 to Day 4, Day 2 to Day 5, and Day 3 to Day 6. Black labels read FIRST THE SPACES and THEN WHAT FILLS THEM.

> On day two, God separates the waters and makes the sky. On day three, the seas pull back, dry land appears, and the ground erupts with plants. Day four brings the sun, the moon and the stars. On day five, the waters fill with creatures, and birds take to the air. On day six, animals cover the land. Watch the pattern. First God makes the spaces, and then he fills them. And again and again comes the same verdict: “it was good.”

## Scene 5: In his own image

**Palette:** teal with a gold glow.
**Visual:** All the creatures from Scene 4 turn toward the center. Two chubby figures appear in a circle of light, labeled THE IMAGE OF GOD with yellow arrows. Stars and whales get small labels reading NOT THIS. Then a halftone cut-out of an ancient royal statue appears, taped beside the figures, with a dotted line connecting them. The whole world then glows gold, and everything stops moving for a moment as Day 7 arrives.
**Strips:** “So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.” (Genesis 1:27) · “and he rested on the seventh day” (Genesis 2:2)

> Then God does something he hasn't done with anything else. “So God created man in his own image, in the image of God created he him; male and female created he them.” Only humans get that label. Not the stars, and not the whales. Scholars point out that in the ancient world, kings set up images of themselves to show who ruled a land. Here, every human being is that image. God looks over all of it. “And, behold, it was very good.” And on day seven, everything goes quiet. “And he rested on the seventh day.”

## Scene 6: Dust and breath

**Palette:** dark earth and teal, with a single thread of white light.
**Visual:** Genesis zooms in. The camera dives from the planet to one patch of ground. Dust particles rise and gather into a chubby paper figure with no face or color yet. A thread of glowing light enters from above. His dot eyes open, and color and aura spread through him. Black labels read DUST and BREATH, with yellow arrows.
**Strip:** “And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.” (Genesis 2:7)

> Now the story zooms in, from the whole universe to one man on the ground. “And the LORD God formed man of the dust of the ground, and breathed into his nostrils the breath of life; and man became a living soul.” Dust and breath. That's what a human being is, in one line. Hold on to that word, dust. It comes back.

## Scene 7: The garden

**Palette:** clip 6's teal map, then bioluminescent Eden.
**Visual:** Use clip 6's map. Neon rivers draw themselves across the dark. Two get labels, TIGRIS (HIDDEKEL) and EUPHRATES. Two get question marks, PISON and GIHON. A red circle and a large question mark sit over the middle, with the black box EDEN'S LOCATION IS UNKNOWN. The circle then becomes a torn-paper window that opens into Eden: glowing trees and fireflies. Two great trees are tagged with labels.
**Strips:** “And the fourth river is Euphrates.” (Genesis 2:14) · “the tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.” (Genesis 2:9)

> God plants a garden in Eden, and puts the man there. A river flows out of it and splits into four. We still know two of the names: the Hiddekel, which is the Tigris, and the Euphrates. The other two, the Pison and the Gihon, can't be placed with any certainty. People have searched for centuries, and nobody knows where Eden was. Inside it stand two trees that matter more than all the rest. “The tree of life also in the midst of the garden, and the tree of knowledge of good and evil.”

## Scene 8: One rule

**Palette:** bright Eden teal.
**Visual:** Dozens of glowing trees fill the screen, and each gets a yellow check mark in quick succession. A counter label runs up: EVERY TREE. One tree remains in the center. A red marker draws one circle around it, and a black label reads ONE.
**Strip:** “Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.” (Genesis 2:16-17)

> The man is given work: to tend the garden and keep it. And he's given one boundary. “Of every tree of the garden thou mayest freely eat: But of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die.” Look at the ratio. Every tree, freely. One tree, no. Remember that, too.

## Scene 9: Not good

**Palette:** Eden teal, cooling slightly, then warm rose.
**Visual:** The man sits alone, small on a huge screen. Red marker strikes through the repeating words GOOD, GOOD, GOOD, and writes NOT GOOD. Animals parade past in stop-motion, each receiving a black name label. None stands beside him. The screen dims for the deep sleep. A soft light. A second figure appears, labeled THE WOMAN, and their two auras merge.
**Strips:** “It is not good that the man should be alone” (Genesis 2:18) · “This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh” (Genesis 2:23)

> Up to now, everything has been good. Now, for the first time, something isn't. “It is not good that the man should be alone.” God brings the animals to the man, and he names every one. A whole parade of creatures, and no match among them. So God puts the man into a deep sleep, takes a rib, and makes a woman. The man's first recorded words are a poem. “This is now bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh.” And for one brief stretch, nothing in the world is wrong. There's no fear, and no shame.

## Scene 10: A question

**Palette:** teal begins bleeding into crimson from the edges of the frame.
**Visual:** All motion slows. Fireflies thin out. A magenta serpent with glowing yellow spots winds down the central tree, labeled THE SERPENT. Grain gets heavier. Its question is typed onto a torn strip, and the words appear one by one.
**Strips:** “Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field” (Genesis 3:1) · “Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?” (Genesis 3:1)

> Then a new character slides into the story. “Now the serpent was more subtil than any beast of the field.” Subtil means cunning. Genesis doesn't stop here to say who or what is behind it. It simply speaks, and its first words are a question. “Yea, hath God said, Ye shall not eat of every tree of the garden?”

## Scene 11: Side by side

**Palette:** crimson on aged paper, in the most document-heavy scene of the film.
**Visual:** Three torn strips line up, labeled GOD, THE SERPENT and THE WOMAN. Red marker circles EVERY and FREELY on the first, and NOT ... EVERY on the second. On the woman's line, TOUCH gets a thick yellow highlight and a black label reading NOT IN GENESIS 2:17. Then a final strip slams on and shakes. A red line links its NOT to the surely die in God's warning.
**Strips:** “Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die.” (Genesis 3:3) · “Ye shall not surely die” (Genesis 3:4) · “ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil” (Genesis 3:5)

> Look at what just happened. Put the sentences side by side. God said every tree, freely, except one. The serpent asks whether God said no trees at all. The woman corrects it, almost. “Ye shall not eat of it, neither shall ye touch it, lest ye die.” Touch it. That isn't in the command as Genesis records it. The serpent hasn't even told a lie yet, and already God sounds stricter than he is. Now comes the lie. “Ye shall not surely die.” And then, the reason it offers. God is holding out on you. “Ye shall be as gods, knowing good and evil.”

## Scene 12: The fruit

**Palette:** full crimson, with the fruit as the brightest thing on screen.
**Visual:** Clip 6's temptation staging. Three black labels pop on one at a time around the fruit: GOOD FOR FOOD, PLEASANT TO THE EYES, TO MAKE ONE WISE. Her arm reaches. Sound-design moment: silence. She takes it and turns. The camera finds the man standing right beside her, and a yellow arrow with a black label reads WITH HER. A shockwave ripples outward from the bite and every glowing thing in Eden goes out, one after another.
**Strip:** “she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.” (Genesis 3:6)

> She looks at it. It's good for food. It's pleasant to the eyes. It's desired to make one wise. And then, in one of the quietest sentences in the Bible, “she took of the fruit thereof, and did eat, and gave also unto her husband with her; and he did eat.” With her. He was right there.

## Scene 13: Hiding

**Palette:** drained crimson sliding toward purple.
**Visual:** Their auras flicker out. Leaf cut-outs are taped over them, slightly crooked, in stop-motion. A soft white light begins moving through the distant trees from the right. They shuffle behind a tree. The camera holds on two pairs of dot eyes peeking out.
**Strip:** “And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked” (Genesis 3:7)

> The serpent was right about one thing. Their eyes do open. “And the eyes of them both were opened, and they knew that they were naked.” They wanted to be like God. What they got was shame. They sew fig leaves together. Then they hear God coming through the garden “in the cool of the day,” and two people who once had nothing to hide do the only thing they can think of. They hide.

## Scene 14: Where art thou?

**Palette:** deep purple, lit by one gentle white light.
**Visual:** The light stops at the tree. Three glowing words form in the dark. Long hold. The man steps out, tiny. Then a blame diagram builds: thick yellow arrows pointing from THE MAN to THE WOMAN, curving up toward the light with a label reading AND GOD, then from THE WOMAN to THE SERPENT. The last arrow from THE SERPENT has nowhere to go, and it curls and stops.
**Strips:** “Where art thou?” (Genesis 3:9) · “I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid” (Genesis 3:10) · “The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat.” (Genesis 3:12) · “The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.” (Genesis 3:13)

> And God asks a question. “Where art thou?” It isn't that he doesn't know. The one who made the stars is giving a man the chance to come out. “I heard thy voice in the garden, and I was afraid.” For the first time, a human being is afraid of God. Then comes the blame. The man points at the woman, and past her, at God. “The woman whom thou gavest to be with me, she gave me of the tree, and I did eat.” The woman points at the serpent. “The serpent beguiled me, and I did eat.” And the serpent has no one left to point at.

## Scene 15: Dust again

**Palette:** cold purple, with cracked earth glowing faint red.
**Visual:** Three panels tear open, one per consequence: the serpent sinking to the ground, the two figures with a crack spreading between them, and the man pushing against a ground that sprouts black paper thorns. The DUST label from Scene 6 slides back on screen. The man's color drains into dust particles, then reforms, breathing but dimmer.
**Strip:** “for dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.” (Genesis 3:19)

> So God speaks to each of them, starting with the serpent. It will crawl on its belly. Childbirth will bring pain, and even the bond between husband and wife will become a struggle. The ground itself will fight back, with thorns and thistles and sweat. And here's that word again. “For dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.” They're still breathing. But death has started its work.

## Scene 16: The promise

**Palette:** dark purple, with the first warm gold of the film's ending.
**Visual:** Rewind to the words spoken to the serpent. One torn strip is pulled out of the pile and taped at the center. Yellow highlighter sweeps across her seed. A tiny gold point of light appears beside the woman and doesn't go out for the rest of the film. The label over her head peels away, THE WOMAN, and a new one is taped on: EVE.
**Strips:** “And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.” (Genesis 3:15) · “And Adam called his wife's name Eve; because she was the mother of all living.” (Genesis 3:20)

> But buried inside the words to the serpent is a strange promise. “And I will put enmity between thee and the woman, and between thy seed and her seed; it shall bruise thy head, and thou shalt bruise his heel.” One day, a descendant of this woman will strike the serpent's head, and be wounded doing it. Christians have long read this as the Bible's first hint of a rescuer. And standing under a sentence of death, the man gives his wife a name full of life. “And Adam called his wife's name Eve; because she was the mother of all living.”

## Scene 17: Coats of skins

**Palette:** purple and gold, in the warmest and stillest scene since Eden.
**Visual:** The crooked taped leaves are peeled off, one at a time. A soft light surrounds them, and two simple warm-colored coats appear on the figures, stitched with a thin gold line. No hands are visible.
**Strip:** “Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them.” (Genesis 3:21)

> Then, before anyone leaves, God does something tender. “Unto Adam also and to his wife did the LORD God make coats of skins, and clothed them.” The leaves they made for themselves are replaced by what God makes for them. Even in judgment, he covers them.

## Scene 18: The gate, again

**Palette:** returns to Scene 1's purple night, with Eve's gold point still glowing.
**Visual:** The tree of life glows far behind them. The gate rises, with wings of light and a turning sword, matching Scene 1 exactly. A yellow arrow tagged WHAT IT GUARDS runs from the sword to the tree of life. Two small figures walk away, leaving footprints across the paper. The camera slides right, past the edge of the wall, to a final torn strip reading GENESIS 1–3. The gold point stays, and it is the last thing to fade.
**Strips:** “lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever” (Genesis 3:22) · “So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.” (Genesis 3:24)

> And then, they have to go. Genesis gives the reason. “Lest he put forth his hand, and take also of the tree of life, and eat, and live for ever.” “So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.” So that's the sword. Not guarding treasure. Guarding the way to life. Two people walk out, into the world we still live in. And nearly every story after this one is about the same question. How do we get back? The Bible doesn't leave that open. On its final pages, there is a tree of life again. And the gates are not shut.
