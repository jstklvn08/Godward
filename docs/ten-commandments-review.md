## Problems that repeat across episodes (fix once, in the shared kit, and every episode improves)

1. **Arms across faces.** A raised hand, a held phone or a prop sits in front of the face (eps 7, 8, 9 and a few others). The
   figure kit lets an arm target land anywhere; the hands should be kept below the chin automatically (clamp arm targets
   that fall inside the head circle).
2. **People cut by the frame edge.** Half-figures at the left or right edge (eps 4, 6, 7, 8). An automatic check could flag
   any figure whose body crosses the visible frame (±462/zoom around the camera centre).
3. **Weak endings.** The "next time" scene in eps 4, 6, 8 is small grey tablets on a dark or pale sky. The last image before
   the outro should be the strongest. Use a single shared ending: the tablets big and glowing over the episode's own
   setting, with that commandment's line lit, the family looking up, and a short pull-back.
4. **The same tall dark grass in the bottom of the frame.** It returns in most outdoor scenes of every episode. Rotate other
   foregrounds: stones and a water jar, flowers, a stream edge, a low wall, animals close to the camera.
5. **Crowded group shots.** Four or five big heads packed together (eps 5, 6, 9). At the big scale, three people is the most
   that reads; show bigger groups as a wide shot or as a two-shot plus people behind.
6. **The back leg never shows.** A small bug in the figure kit gives every person's far leg an invalid colour, so it isn't
   drawn. Fixing it adds the second leg everywhere; this needs a re-render of all episodes, so fix it with the next round.
7. **Small symbolic objects in big empty frames.** Openings and "idea" shots like the dagger sign (ep 5), the rings on the
   shelf (ep 7) and the hand in a gold circle (ep 8) read as logos. Make the object the hero: big, lit, with a person's hands
   on it.

## Ideas to make the series stronger (beyond fixing faults)

- **Hook in the first 2 seconds.** Every episode should open on movement and a face, not on a still object: the man staring
  at the house (10), the carpenter's axe mid-swing (2), the brothers walking out at dusk (6).
- **One recurring family across the series.** The same parents, child and grandfather appear in each episode at Sinai and in
  the "today" scenes. Viewers get attached, and the series feels connected.
- **Sound.** Give each commandment its own short musical motif, played when its tablet line lights. Add a heartbeat under
  the "heart" moments (7, 10) and a crowd murmur at the city gate (9).
- **Captions.** For Shorts and Reels, a bigger first-three-seconds caption with the question ("Which commandment comes
  first?") helps retention.
- **Thumbnails for every episode** in the approved poster style (ep 1 and ep 10): the tablets glowing over the episode's
  setting, that commandment's line burning, small figures, a short headline.
- **Series order and length.** At about 4 minutes each, these suit YouTube. For TikTok and Reels, cut a 60–75-second version
  of each: the hook, the story beat, the verse, the one-line takeaway.
