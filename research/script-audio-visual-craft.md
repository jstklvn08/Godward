# How explainer channels build their videos: ranked for Godward

Research date: 2026-11-10. Two research passes, one on script and audio, one on visuals, studying Kurzgesagt, BibleProject, Vox, Johnny Harris, TED-Ed, CGP Grey, Veritasium, Bible Animations and others. Duplicates between the two passes are merged here.

**Scores out of 10.** **I** = impact on retention. **E** = ease for a solo creator with code-based animation and Chatterbox TTS. **O** = overall, weighted toward impact. Tags: 🎙 script/audio, 🎨 visual, ⚙ process.

**Heads-up.** BibleProject released its own Ten Commandments series ("Ten Words") in 2026, with a paper-and-highlighter look. That is Godward's closest competitor on both topic and style (see #22).

## What each one means for Godward

**1. Hook on the title's promise.** Ask the title's question with an image inside ~20 seconds, with motion from the first frame and a question label within ~5 seconds. YouTube's Intro metric measures who is still watching at 0:30. Both commandment cold opens already do this; keep the first line under ten words. *Sources: YouTube Help key moments; MrBeast production memo.*

**2. Grey-box animatic.** TTS makes scratch narration free. Render each draft as grey cards, labels and wall camera moves, watch it on a phone, show it to 2–3 people, fix, then make the art. BibleProject says its storyboards "almost always" force a rewrite. *Sources: Kurzgesagt process; TED-Ed animator; Torah Visuals; BibleProject interviews.*

**3. Misconception first.** In Veritasium's PhD study, voicing the common wrong idea and then correcting it nearly doubled learning versus a clear explanation. Part 1 should name the misconception in the first minute ("Most people picture Israel swapping God for an idol") so Aaron's "feast to the LORD" pays it off. *Source: Derek Muller, Veritasium.*

**4. But/therefore spine.** Every beat joins the next with "but" or "therefore", never "and then". Part 1 passes: rescued, BUT forty days of silence, THEREFORE "make us gods", BUT a feast to the LORD, THEREFORE broken tablets. *Source: Trey Parker and Matt Stone.*

**5. "Notice" moments.** One checkable detail per act, revealed with a pause and the highlighter. "Before" = "face" and the carve/cut verb are Godward's signature moments. Cite the lexicon in the description. *Sources: BibleProject on repeated key words.*

**6. Two-column script.** Turn each Visual block into rows: line · cue word · change · asset (reuse or new) · ★hero. Aim for a meaningful change every 3–5 seconds and a new sheet every 30–45 seconds. *Sources: Vox-style teaching guide; Johnny Harris; Kurzgesagt (~200 panels a video).*

**7. Re-hooks.** Plan a question line and a visual event near 1:45, 3:30 and 5:30–6:00, each marked by a music change. Part 2's "So why does that matter so much?" is the model. Close every loop you open. *Sources: Loewenstein's curiosity gap; MrBeast memo.*

**8. Build narration line by line.** One or two sentences at a time, 3–5 seeds each, keep the best, log seed and preset per line. Chatterbox's README calls it retired research since August 2026, so pin the version and keep the cached weights. *Sources: Kurzgesagt's narrator; Chatterbox README.*

**9. Lock audio, then time to word cues.** Align the final TTS with WhisperX into `cues.json`, and have scenes call `cue('carved')` instead of seconds typed by hand. A new narration then retimes the whole video. This flips the clips' current method, where narration follows the `mount(...)` timings. *Sources: Kurzgesagt; TED-Ed; Mayer's multimedia principles; WhisperX.*

**10. Write for the ear.** One-breath sentences (~20 words max), active verbs, concrete images, rounded numbers, no brackets. At 140 words a minute, a 6–9-minute video is about 800–1,200 words including verses. *Sources: radio-script practice; Kurzgesagt's ~1,300-word scripts.*

**11. Pause and emphasis markers.** Formalise the existing `[pause]`: [beat] 0.3 s, [pause] 0.8 s, [hold] 1.5 s. The renderer inserts the silence; the tags never reach the model. Build emphasis into sentences ("Same words. Second chance.") rather than capitals. *Sources: Chatterbox pull request #164; third-party Chatterbox guide.*

**12. Voice the viewer's question.** The single narrator plays student and teacher: three or four question lines per episode, rendered a little warmer. This replaces BibleProject's second host. *Sources: BibleProject; Veritasium's dialogue result.*

**13. Marker writes itself on.** A study of 6.9 million edX sessions found hand-drawn tutorials held viewers 1.5–2× longer than slides. Make drawing-on the default for coral circles, arrows and Hebrew words; keep the slap-on entrance for black labels only. *Source: Guo, Kim and Rubin (2014).*

**14. Hero-scene budget.** 2–3 ★hero beats per episode get bespoke art and continuous motion (the calf, the breaking, the shining face). Everything else uses held poses, paper slaps and camera moves. *Sources: Kurzgesagt sketch stage; BibleProject.*

**15. Wall laid out like the text.** Days 1–3 above days 4–6; the commandments as two tablets, 1–4 toward God and 5–10 toward neighbour. The closing pull-back doubles as recap, end screen, thumbnail base and a free poster. *Sources: BibleProject visual parallelism and poster endings.*

**16. Metaphors from Scripture.** Keep a metaphor bank of images the Bible itself uses: the smoking firepot of Genesis 15 for covenant, Isaiah 44's half-log for idolatry, a king's statue for the image of God. *Sources: BibleProject's candle for loyal love; Torah Visuals.*

**17. Camera grammar.** Zoom into the Sinai flame and out onto the next sheet; pull back from one figure to the whole wall; match-cut on shape (fruit to sun); give each sheet 3–4 parallax layers. *Sources: Powers of Ten; BibleProject; Vox transitions.*

**18. Talk to one person.** "You", "we", contractions, a guide's voice rather than an oracle's. Conversational wording beat formal wording in 10 of 10 of Mayer's tests. Watch the application sections for drift into preaching. *Sources: Mayer; Johnny Harris.*

**19. Separate spoken and on-screen text.** Generate a spoken layer automatically: LORD → Lord (test it), numerals → words, a pronunciation list for Aaron, Sinai, Horeb and Hebrew terms. `render_full.py`'s `SAY` table is the start of this.

**20. Say how certain each claim is.** "The text says", "many scholars read this as", "Christians differ here". Add a five-second note on commandment numbering in part 2. *Sources: Kurzgesagt; BibleProject; Logos on numbering.*

**21. End by answering the title.** One-line answer, call back the opening image, widen out, tease the next episode. Part 2 does this; part 1 should repeat its answer in the closing lines.

**22. Visual lexicon, and look different from BibleProject.** One page: hard gold with no glow = idol; glow = life, presence or God's image; coral = narrator's reasoning; yellow = Scripture; black label = a claim; a tear = a breach. BibleProject's Ten Words uses crumpled paper and a flip that turns each prohibition into its positive, so Godward should use its own devices, such as backlit paper revealing hidden writing and tears mended with glowing tape.

**23. Ration the sacred light.** Divine light is the only light that falls on the paper around it; other glows stay self-contained. No lens flares, pastels or photoreal faces. *Sources: BibleProject's "never went 3D unless" rule; Aleph Beta; criticism of AI Bible videos.*

**24. Breathers and chapters.** Hold 1.5–3 seconds after key verses with only paper jitter and dust moving. Each cluster of sheets becomes a YouTube chapter (first at 00:00, at least three, each 10 s or longer). *Sources: Joss Fong; YouTube chapters.*

**25. Scripture-on-screen rules.** Keep the torn cream strip, serif type and black reference label. Show the verse while it's read, sweep only the key phrase, three lines maximum.

**26. Code kit and wall spec.** Document `style-kit.js` as an API, add a palette file, a character registry and a JSON wall spec of sheets, positions and cues. Make 2–3 new hero assets per episode so nothing looks templated. *Sources: Kurzgesagt; BobbyBroccoli.*

**27. One theme, ruthless cuts.** Put the one-sentence thesis at the top of each script; every section must justify itself against it. *Sources: BibleProject; Kurzgesagt's dozen drafts; CGP Grey.*

**28. TTS preset per beat, fixed Scripture sound.** `warm` for hooks and twists, `default` for explanation, `restrained` for verses, `calm` for landings. For every verse, dip the music a further ~6 dB, add a soft tape sound, and leave 0.4 s before and 0.8 s after, so viewers can hear Scripture apart from paraphrase.

**29. Score by section, and use silence.** Three to five cues that change at the turns. Part 1: awe, unease, a brittle shimmer at the calf, silence as the tablets break, a warm return. One Godward motif on every end card. *Sources: Kurzgesagt and Epic Mountain; Every Frame a Painting.*

**30. Point to God, without moralising.** Applications land on God's character, not "be good" lessons. Part 2 should note that Catholic and Orthodox Christians distinguish venerating images from worshipping them. *Sources: Phil Vischer on VeggieTales; BibleProject; Kurzgesagt.*

**31. The wall remembers.** A standard word card (Hebrew, transliteration, meaning) with coral lines to every sheet where the word recurs; motifs across episodes (flaming sword, bush, Sinai, pillar of fire); one hidden object per episode.

**32. Style frames, colour script, thumbnail.** Finish 2–3 hero frames before animating; they double as thumbnail candidates (one glowing object on dark paper, a black label of three words at most). Render one frame per sheet into a strip and check the colour follows the emotional arc. Test three thumbnails with YouTube's Test & Compare.

**33. Mix to targets.** Match loudness across TTS chunks, master to −14 to −16 LUFS with true peaks at or below −1 dBTP, music 18–25 dB under the voice. If you can follow the melody under the narration, the music is too loud.

**34. One reusable map sheet.** An Ancient Near East module from public-domain Natural Earth outlines, with paper texture, routes that draw themselves on and pinned labels. Reuse it for Eden, Abraham, the Exodus and the exile. *Source: Johnny Harris.*

**35. Written depiction policy.** Keep God as fire, cloud, light and words. Decide now how to show Jesus (an ordinary figure, or face never shown). Show violence through absence: a cut-out tips over, a glow goes out, the camera holds on a witness.

**36. Outside readers and a public source list.** Two readers from other traditions per script; every verse and commentary listed in the description. *Sources: The Chosen's consultants; Kurzgesagt; Vox.*

**37. Inauthentic-content rule.** Since July 2025 YouTube won't monetise templated, mass-produced videos. Vary hooks and structures between episodes. A short "synthetic narration" credit would build trust.

**38. Modern bridge and humour.** In "today" sections, switch materials (newsprint, receipts, phone cut-outs) and put figures in modern clothes. Aim humour at human folly ("SAME TREE"), never at God.

**39. License music safely.** Start with the YouTube Audio Library plus one subscription (Epidemic Sound or Artlist cover only videos published while subscribed), keep a log per video, and commission an original theme later.

## One combined workflow

1. **Series bible (once):** visual lexicon, palette, character registry, metaphor bank, depiction policy, kit API, pronunciation list.
2. **Question, thesis, misconception:** the title question, a one-sentence answer, and the wrong idea it overturns.
3. **Research:** two or three translations, two commentaries from different traditions, a lexicon for any Hebrew claim; log every claim.
4. **Talk it through** aloud with a friend for 30–60 minutes and note every question they ask.
5. **Beat sheet:** 8–12 beats joined by but/therefore, with the misconception payoff, one "notice" per act, re-hooks every ~2 minutes and the final answer.
6. **Two-column draft** with cue words and ★hero beats, within the word budget.
7. **Ear pass and TTS table read.** Rewrite wherever attention drifts.
8. **Accuracy and tradition pass,** then outside readers.
9. **Grey-box animatic** on scratch TTS, watched on a phone by 2–3 people. Cut what the pictures already say.
10. **Lock the audio:** spoken layer, 3–5 seeds per line, explicit silences, matched loudness, WhisperX cues.
11. **Style frames,** then the art pass on the same layout.
12. **Checks:** colour strip, glow budget, readable Scripture, re-hooks near 3:00 and 6:00.
13. **Music and effects last,** mixed to −14 to −16 LUFS.
14. **Publish:** chapters, captions from the script, sources and copyright notice in the description, three thumbnails tested.
15. **Learn:** at 48–72 hours, match each retention dip to its sheet and write one lesson into a running notes file.

## Benchmarks

- Kurzgesagt: about 1,200 hours per video, with 2–3 illustrators and 2–3 animators over 8–12 weeks.
- Torah Visuals: about 400 hours per 3-minute video.
- CGP Grey, solo: 10–20 hours per finished minute.
- Full studios work out at roughly 100–150 hours per finished minute. A solo pipeline only works through reuse, hero budgeting and code.

## Gaps

Neither pass found primary sources on how Wendover, Overly Sarcastic Productions, PolyMatter, Oversimplified or The Infographics Show work. Several Chatterbox tips come from third-party guides and need testing on this setup.
